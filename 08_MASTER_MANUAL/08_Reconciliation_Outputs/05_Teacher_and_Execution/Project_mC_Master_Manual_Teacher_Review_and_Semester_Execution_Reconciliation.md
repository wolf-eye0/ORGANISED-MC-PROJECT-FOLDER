## 2. Reconciliation method

This reconciliation applies the attached authority hierarchy in the following order: current teacher requirements and formally recorded approvals; Decision Register v1.2; final Phase 3C architecture reports; final concept memories and SOPs; comparative gates and uncertainty records; valid syntheses and adjudications; controlled research lanes; historical project records; and finally manifests, launchers and archived transport material. A lower-authority presentation, draft, roadmap, generated visual, model output or repository index cannot silently override a higher-authority project record.

Five evidence states are kept separate:

| Label | Meaning in this reconciliation |
|---|---|
| **CURRENT FACT** | Directly supported current project state or frozen architecture boundary in validated Parts I–IV |
| **OPEN DECISION** | A required decision for which no controlling approval or result is attached |
| **REQUIRED ACTION** | A step that must occur before a later claim, purchase, build stage or validation stage becomes supportable |
| **PROPOSAL** | A planning structure, target, roadmap step, role map, threshold or fallback that is not yet approved or completed |
| **PROHIBITED CLAIM** | A statement that the attached record does not support and that must not appear as fact |

The following rules control both chapters:

- A presentation proves what was submitted or prepared for teacher-facing review; it does not prove teacher approval, implementation or successful validation.
- A change request proves that a change was requested; it does not prove that the change was accepted.
- A roadmap is conditional planning. It becomes an implementation plan only after the relevant concept, platform and scope are authorized.
- A generated visual may establish provenance, but it cannot establish technical truth.
- Model recurrence and adjudication are decision support, not votes.
- Historical prices and specifications remain dated planning evidence. Current manufacturer specifications, Indian prices, seller authenticity and availability require a later controlled verification pass.
- The ordinary semester MVPs are not established as patentable. D11, VPD-C7, PIRG and TrueMoist replacement/remount/transfer mechanisms remain separate future research hypotheses.
- Missing conclusions are not reconstructed. Where the source set contains no teacher feedback, no execution record or no measured result, the reconciliation records the absence.

## 10.4 What the presentation proposed

The official presentation proposed a bounded assistive-technology prototype rather than a completed product.

1. **Motivation.** It described commercial refreshable Braille systems as expensive because of precision actuation and manufacturing, identified the literacy value of tactile Braille, and framed access in schools, libraries and resource-constrained institutions as the problem context.
2. **Semester objective.** It proposed checking whether affordable electromechanical components could create refreshable tactile output in one six-dot cell.
3. **Scale choice.** It explicitly stated that the semester prototype would be macro-scaled to reduce ordinary FDM tolerance problems.
4. **Mechanism.** It proposed six SG90-class micro servos, six eccentric cams and six tactile pins.
5. **Controller and interface.** It proposed an ESP32-WROOM-32 controller producing six servo-control signals and accepting a text/USB path.
6. **Power concept.** It proposed an external 5 V servo rail with common ground and a planned 1,000 µF bulk capacitor.
7. **Fabrication.** It proposed using college FDM printing and readily available parts.
8. **Budget.** It reported a historical estimated BOM range of ₹2,060–₹2,850, subject to part prices and design changes.
9. **Validation sequence.** It proposed beginning with one dot and proceeding step by step to a complete six-dot cell after basic validation.
10. **Future direction.** It stated that multi-cell work and possible IP options might be explored after experiments.

These are proposal statements. The deck does not contain physical test results, procurement records, current quotations, completed tactile evaluation or evidence that a six-dot device was built.

## 11.16 Stop, failure and scope-reduction rules

The following rules control execution:

1. **Authorization stop:** no procurement or Phase 4 claim before concept and platform authorization.
2. **Safety stop:** stop immediately on unsafe motion, heating, exposed pinch/rotating hazard, unstable power or facility-rule breach.
3. **Gate stop:** do not expand a failed subsystem into full integration; correct, narrow or report failure.
4. **No silent substitution:** a board, actuator, sensor, mechanism, model, sample plan or power design change requires change control.
5. **Budget stop:** if the verified BOM exceeds the approved ceiling, reduce scope or obtain explicit exception; do not hide institutional or borrowed dependencies.
6. **Schedule stop:** drop optional FFT/Mahalanobis/display, multi-cell expansion, advanced model terms or future patent features before compromising baseline evidence.
7. **Held-out stop:** no threshold/model retuning after opening the locked holdout. A failed holdout remains a result.
8. **Facility stop:** if required FDM, tactile-protocol approval, oven, scale or safe rig access is unavailable, use the documented bounded fallback or do not make the dependent claim.
9. **Evidence stop:** no final metric without raw data, identifiers, configuration and reproducible analysis.
10. **Scope-reduction rule:** any reduction—such as OpenBraille one-dot result, VibeGuard baseline-only result or TrueMoist 54-run contingency—must be authorized, explicitly labelled and reflected in the claim boundary.
