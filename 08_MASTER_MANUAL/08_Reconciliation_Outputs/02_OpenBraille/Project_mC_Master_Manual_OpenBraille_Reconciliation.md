# Chapter 04 — OpenBraille Frozen Architecture and Semester Scope

## 04.1 Controlling architecture identity

The controlling OpenBraille semester architecture is:

- one permanent macro-scale six-dot tactile cell;
- six SG90-class micro servos, one independently controlled actuator per dot;
- six eccentric cams or equivalent frozen cam/follower elements;
- six guided vertical tactile pins with safe rounded contact tips;
- local ESP32 control using six direct PWM signal channels;
- an FDM-manufactured frame, guides, cams/followers and related mechanical structure;
- external regulated servo power, electrically separated from the laptop USB power path while sharing the required signal reference ground;
- USB/serial command input and evidence logging;
- single-dot-first mechanical and electrical commissioning before full-cell integration; and
- active servo-driven raise and retract positions unless a usable passive return is physically demonstrated in the procured, coupled mechanism.

This identity is a non-standard educational tactile/mechanical proof-of-concept. It is not an ISO-compliant cell, a commercial refreshable Braille display, a fluent-reading device, a validated assistive product or a patent-ready mechanism.

The Phase 3C report and memory remain the authoritative architecture sources, but their passive-reset language is overridden by the higher-authority active-retraction rule preserved in Decision Register v1.2, the patent syntheses, the pre-build simulation, the current reference dossier and validated Part I.

## 04.2 Project purpose and bounded semester objective

OpenBraille is an accessibility-oriented embedded/mechatronics experiment intended to test whether inexpensive commodity actuation and accessible fabrication can produce one repeatable refreshable tactile cell. The semester objective is narrow: accept a character or six-dot pattern through USB/serial, command six independent servo/cam channels, physically raise and actively retract the intended dots, record the commanded and measured evidence, and disclose the prototype’s limitations.

The project does not have to reproduce commercial Braille dimensions or support fluent reading to satisfy its bounded educational purpose. Macro scaling is the selected way to reduce FDM tolerance risk. That scaling choice validates buildability only at the tested educational geometry; it cannot establish commercial-cell density, force, thickness, speed, noise, durability, accessibility performance or standards compliance.

OpenBraille remains a legitimate retained Phase 3C concept and a complete accessibility/mechatronics path. Validated Part I nevertheless records VibeGuard as the strongest semester-build recommendation and states that the requested shift from OpenBraille to VibeGuard is not proved teacher-approved. Therefore this chapter may define the complete OpenBraille fallback/retained implementation, but it must not state that OpenBraille is the currently authorized semester build, that procurement is frozen, or that Phase 4 has started.

## 04.3 Functional architecture

The reconciled functional chain is:

1. A host computer sends a supported character, six-bit dot pattern, clear command, test command or calibration command through USB serial.
2. The ESP32 validates and parses the request.
3. A versioned Braille mapping table converts the supported character into a six-dot target pattern.
4. The controller compares the target pattern with the commanded state and applies per-channel up or down PWM values through a fixed, bounded sequence.
5. Each servo rotates its eccentric cam; the cam/follower relationship converts rotary motion into guided vertical pin travel.
6. A raised command moves the relevant pin toward its defined raised band. A lowered command actively drives the channel toward its defined retracted band.
7. Firmware records the command, channel, calibration/configuration ID, sequence timing, resets and reported faults.
8. External measurement during development determines whether the physical pin state was actually correct. Without qualified direct pin-side evidence, runtime may report `COMMAND_FINISHED` but not `UPPER_CONFIRMED` or `LOWER_CONFIRMED`.

The ordinary semester architecture does not include a permanent endpoint-sensing array. Current/voltage observations may support power and safety analysis, but they cannot certify pin height or tactile correctness. Physical truth comes from controlled external displacement measurement and the approved tactile protocol.

## 04.4 Mechanical architecture

The mechanical system is one macro six-dot cell assembled from six independently testable channels. Each channel contains an SG90-class servo, a servo horn or coupling, an eccentric cam, a follower relationship, a vertically guided pin and structural features that maintain alignment. The shared FDM frame holds the six channels at the selected macro pitch and provides guide support, mounting, guarding and cable management.

The architecture report used approximately 300% of a cited standard sign-Braille scale, approximately 7 mm dot pitch and approximately 1.5 mm raised travel as design starting points. These dimensions are historical engineering targets, not a standards declaration and not measured performance. Final CAD must be labelled non-standard. Printed tolerance coupons, actual procured-part dimensions and measured travel must control final clearances.

The raised state is a physically measured band in which a pin is sufficiently protruded and stable for the approved macro tactile test. The lowered state is a physically measured retracted band that avoids unintended tactile presentation and permits the next pattern. Neither state may be inferred solely from servo angle, PWM pulse width, elapsed time or visible cam movement.

Return motion is active. Firmware commands and the cam geometry must drive both raising and lowering. A passive spring, gravity return or internal servo effect may be used only after physical inspection and loaded tests show that it produces reliable return in the complete coupled mechanism. Until that proof exists, passive return remains an unverified assumption and must not appear in the final chapter as an architecture fact.

Single-dot-first construction is mandatory. The team should print and test a one-channel coupon before committing the full frame and servo batch. Guide clearance, pin tilt, cam/follower continuity, active retraction, servo stall, pinch hazards and repeatability must pass at one dot before six-dot expansion.

Allowed mechanical development work includes tolerance coupons, measured sanding or finishing, print-orientation changes, material changes within the FDM architecture, cam-profile iteration, rounded-tip refinement, guarded covers, replaceable test channels and revised clearances. Every change must retain the frozen servo-per-dot cam/follower principle and must be versioned. A change to another actuation principle or a different OpenBraille architecture requires formal change control.

## 04.5 Electronics and control

The baseline controller is an ESP32-WROOM-32-class development board using direct PWM outputs to the six servo signal inputs. The exact GPIO map in the reference dossier is provisional and must be revalidated against the purchased board. Boot-strapping pins, input-only pins and peripheral conflicts must be avoided. The architecture does not require an H-bridge, Darlington array, shift register or intermediate motor-driver stage for SG90 signal control because the servo electronics are integrated into each actuator.

USB has two roles: serial command input and evidence logging. It is not the six-servo power source. The servo rail must be supplied by an external regulated 5 V-class source through suitable conductors and distribution. The logic/USB domain and servo power branch must share the intended reference ground, while the servo current path must not pass through the ESP32 3.3 V regulator or the laptop USB cable. Bulk capacitance near the servo distribution is an allowed transient-control measure, but it does not replace an adequate supply.

The Phase 3C architecture named a 5 V/2 A adapter and 1,000 µF capacitor. Later controlled planning uses a 5 V/3 A procurement target with acceptance determined by measured voltage droop, resets, heating and worst planned transitions. The exact required current is not frozen by calculation alone. The final chapter should therefore state “external regulated servo power, provisionally 5 V with adequate measured current headroom” and preserve 2 A and 3 A as historical/provisional planning figures until measured testing and fresh specification verification are complete.

A fixed sequential or bounded-group transition policy is permitted to reduce peak current and improve diagnostic clarity. It is an implementation refinement, not proof of invention. The final sequence must be frozen before locked validation and must meet an approved refresh-time limit without brownouts, unsafe heating or hidden failures.

## 04.6 Firmware and operating sequence

The semester firmware should be deterministic, versioned and deliberately modest. The reconciled modules are:

- configuration manager for board variant, GPIO map, build ID, channel identity, calibration values and transition policy;
- USB/serial parser for character, dot-pattern, clear, test and calibration commands;
- unit-tested character-to-six-dot mapping table;
- PWM/channel manager for per-dot up/down command values;
- calibration manager that stores values derived from external physical measurement;
- pattern-transition engine that changes only required dots and applies the frozen sequencing policy;
- fault/log manager for invalid commands, resets, timeouts, manual faults, retries and isolated channels; and
- operator-controlled test mode for one-channel cycles, repeatability runs and lifecycle runs.

A normal operating sequence is:

1. boot the ESP32 with the servo rail initially controlled according to the approved wiring procedure;
2. emit the firmware, board, build, CAD and calibration identifiers through USB serial;
3. initialize all channels to a safe commanded state;
4. accept and validate a host command;
5. calculate the intended six-dot pattern;
6. actuate the changed channels using the frozen sequence and bounded timing;
7. record command completion, reset events and declared faults;
8. preserve `UNKNOWN` or incomplete status when physical evidence is absent or conflicting; and
9. avoid automatic repeated retries unless a bounded policy has been physically qualified.

The Phase 3C report’s boot “self-test” language must be narrowed. A command sweep can show that the ESP32 emitted PWM and that a servo appeared to move; it cannot verify full pin travel, correct tactile height or complete retraction without independent physical evidence. A software timeout also cannot prove that a servo settled. The final chapter should distinguish software command/communication tests from external mechanical-state validation.

## 04.7 Build materials and manufacturing approach

The primary manufacturing route is institutional or otherwise accessible FDM printing. The architecture report identifies ABS for the frame and PETG/Nylon-class material for cams/followers as candidate selections, with stainless-steel pins and conventional fasteners, wiring, headers and guards. These are design recommendations, not validated material specifications.

The build process should preserve:

- versioned CAD and print files;
- print material, layer settings, orientation, infill and machine identification;
- measured post-print dimensions and clearances;
- every sanding, drilling, lubrication or manual-fit operation;
- part revision, assembly operator and replacement history;
- photographs tied to the configuration ID; and
- failed coupons and reprints rather than only the successful final part.

Dependable FDM access is a project gate. The conservative cost envelope remains below ₹5,000 only when institutional printing is available and reprints are controlled. If the primary FDM path cannot meet the one-dot gate, the correct response is to iterate the FDM coupon or seek authorized change control. The final manual should not silently replace the frozen FDM structure with a different production architecture.

## 04.9 Minimum demonstrable success

The draft-safe minimum demonstrable success is:

> One non-standard macro six-dot cell, built within the approved budget and schedule, accepts a controlled character or dot pattern through USB/serial; independently commands all six servo/cam/pin channels; actively raises and retracts the intended dots; reproduces the supported patterns without unsafe power behavior; and supplies measured mechanical, electrical, firmware and tactile evidence under a preregistered protocol while reporting all limitations and failures.

The attached architecture-level targets propose:

- all six channels independently raise and actively retract;
- no brownout, unsafe voltage collapse, connector heating or servo stall in the locked sequence;
- 100% correct mapping for the frozen supported pattern set;
- position variation at or below 0.1 mm over a 100-cycle measured set;
- at least 1,000 actuations as an early lifecycle screen; and
- a teacher-approved blinded tactile threshold and trial count fixed before participant testing.

These numbers are proposed acceptance criteria, not achieved results. The tactile threshold is explicitly unresolved. A successful visual demonstration alone does not satisfy tactile success. If approved tactile participation cannot be obtained, the project must report tactile validation as unavailable rather than substitute team-member visual judgement or claim user validation.

## 05.6 D11 hypothesis

### Exact mechanism as supported

D11 is a proposed **mechanically referenced, individually calibrated, sensor-minimal tactile-state verification and bounded-recovery architecture** using the existing six-servo/cam/pin channels. Its asserted full relationship is:

1. an upper mechanical datum defines permitted raised protrusion and a lower datum/cam relationship defines complete active retraction;
2. commissioning finds a minimum reliable raise and retract command region for each channel with a margin that avoids continuous hard stall;
3. one diagnostic channel moves or seats at a time while a shared, sufficiently fast current/voltage measurement captures the response;
4. the response is labelled during development using independent physical displacement/force ground truth;
5. the system may classify a transition as completed, incomplete/jammed or `UNKNOWN`, but must not equate a command or current spike with correct tactile state;
6. at most a bounded low-energy retry is permitted, after which the failed channel is isolated and the cell state is reported honestly;
7. update scheduling remains within the measured supply budget and avoids obscuring diagnostic attribution; and
8. calibration/verification is repeated after replacement, remount, rebuild or significant signature drift.

### Relationship to the ordinary MVP

D11 is not part of the frozen semester architecture. It retains the MVP’s servo/cam/pin channels but adds a new datum, instrumentation, calibration/state logic and fault-recovery evidence programme. It may be tested as a separate bounded overlay after the one-dot and six-dot MVP gates, but it must be separately identified and must not compromise the semester build, budget or schedule.

### Evidence required

D11 requires, at minimum:

- direct displacement and preferably force ground truth;
- labelled normal, incomplete, jam and ambiguous events;
- held-out testing separated from development data;
- comparisons against calibrated open loop, datum-only, current-only and fixed-sequence baselines;
- ablation showing whether each claimed indispensable element materially contributes;
- transfer across servo lots, replacement, reprints, remount/reassembly, voltage, temperature, load, friction, contamination and wear;
- bounded safety/energy evidence for retries and isolation;
- practical-scale single-dot evidence before broad mechanical claims; and
- official Indian-file retrieval, refreshed professional claims/NPL search and institutional IPR review.

The primary synthesis proposed stringent research thresholds, including held-out sensitivity/specificity, zero silently accepted wrong states in a 3,000-transition gate, material improvement beyond partial configurations and practical-scale transfer. These are pre-build research gates, not established project requirements or achieved results.

### Failure conditions

D11 fails as a patent direction if current predicts only generic servo effort, if a current-only completion label produces a silent wrong state, if the full system merely equals the best partial baseline, if thresholds require ad hoc retuning, if replacement/reassembly breaks the result, if practical-scale transfer fails, if safety/latency is unacceptable, if a closer reference is found, or if confidentiality is lost before review.

### What remains unproved

No D11 hardware is attached. No datum, waveform, classifier, state logic, ablation, replacement test, practical-scale coupon or held-out dataset has been demonstrated. No cooperative or unexpected physical effect is established. Novelty, inventive step, eligibility, useful claim scope, FTO and filing value remain unresolved.

**Source basis:** Work Max synthesis (**TIER_2**, D11 definition and stringent gate); Valid Secondary synthesis (**TIER_2**, integrated Track B formulation); pre-build simulation (**TIER_2**, aggregation attack and reduced-D11 comparison).

## 05.7 VPD-C7 hypothesis

### Exact mechanism as supported

VPD-C7 is a proposed **Verified Pin-Datum Cell** developed on a separate post-MVP guide/pin coupon. Its defining technical question is whether the same pin-side mechanical datum that sets an upper or lower tactile endpoint can also generate direct, low-energy endpoint evidence with less registration error and fewer silent wrong states than a passive stop plus a separately mounted sensor.

The supported non-claim mechanism is:

1. redesign one guide/pin coupon with mechanically registered upper and lower endpoint features;
2. arrange each endpoint feature so reaching it can create a low-energy pin-side event without routing servo power through the contact;
3. use independent external displacement and force measurement as the development oracle;
4. use a separate current/voltage channel only for effort, overload, obstruction-like behaviour or supply disturbance—not endpoint certification;
5. declare upper/lower state only from qualified pin-side endpoint evidence;
6. classify absent or conflicting evidence as incomplete, jammed or `UNKNOWN`;
7. permit only physically qualified bounded recovery and channel isolation; and
8. preserve the existing independent servo/cam/pin actuation while redesigning the guide/pin/datum on the separate coupon.

The pre-build comparison models VPD-C7 as twelve endpoint contacts—upper and lower per pin—plus one shared current/voltage channel. That exact count is a simulation architecture, not a frozen implementation mandate or filing-ready claim.

### Relationship to the ordinary MVP

VPD-C7 is outside the semester architecture. It retains the OpenBraille tactile-pin objective and can use the frozen MVP as an engineering baseline, but it changes the guide/pin/datum relationship and adds pin-side endpoint evidence. It therefore belongs in a separate post-semester configuration, folder, budget and test programme. C7 results must never be represented as results of the frozen cell.

### Evidence required

VPD-C7 requires:

- passive-datum-only, separate-direct-sensor, stop-contact-without-current, current-without-contact and full-C7 baselines;
- measured endpoint registration error, force, friction, bounce, contact resistance, debounce, false endpoint rate and energy;
- practical-scale packing and manufacturability evidence;
- wear, contamination, cleaning and lifecycle tests;
- blind servo/cam/pin/datum replacement and independent reassembly trials;
- direct comparison with a passive stop plus separately mounted optical/magnetic/mechanical sensor;
- a locked held-out transition test with no silently accepted wrong tactile states;
- evidence that current adds an independent diagnostic role or removal of current from the prospective mechanism;
- complete dimensions, materials, circuitry, state chart, thresholds, negative results and reproduction instructions; and
- refreshed worldwide claim search, official `5559/CHE/2014` file, inventorship/disclosure audit and target-country FTO review.

### Failure conditions

VPD-C7 fails if the same-datum contact has no measurable advantage over a separate sensor, adds unacceptable friction/force/bounce/wear/debris sensitivity, cannot fit practical geometry, fails after replacement/reassembly, produces a silent accepted wrong state, depends on current to certify endpoint, or collapses to exact hobby parts/macroscale dimensions with trivial scope. It also fails as a filing route if closer prior art is found or professional/institutional review does not justify cost and disclosure risk.

### What remains unproved

No VPD-C7 datum/contact has been built or tested. The familiar nature of stops and switches creates a strong obviousness risk. There is no evidence of lower registration error, fewer silent failures, practical-scale fit, lifecycle durability, a non-additive effect, novelty, inventive step, useful scope or FTO.

**Source basis:** pre-build simulation (**TIER_2**, C7 mechanism, attacks, evidence roadmap and final recommendation); current reference dossier (**TIER_2**, non-claim mechanism, semester separation and confidentiality controls).

## 05.8 Relationship between D11 and VPD-C7

- **Historically sequential:** D11 emerged from the two valid syntheses as the strongest/least-weak integrated direction. The later pre-build simulation attacked D11 and proposed VPD-C7 as a more coherent redesign hypothesis.

## 05.12 Confidentiality and institutional IPR requirements

## 05.13 Filing or no-filing decision boundary

The current decision is **no filing commitment**.

A later counsel-led filing evaluation may be opened only if all of the following are satisfied:

1. the frozen MVP succeeds as an engineering baseline without architecture substitution;
2. a specifically defined future mechanism is actually built and enabled;
3. the mechanism produces a preregistered, reproducible physical effect against strong baselines;
4. any asserted indispensable element survives ablation;
5. the effect transfers across replacement, reassembly, ordinary environmental variation and practical geometry;
6. no locked test produces a silently accepted wrong tactile state;
7. the official Indian close-prior-art file and a refreshed worldwide claim-language search are resolved;
8. the actual geometry, circuitry, firmware and intended jurisdictions receive patentability and FTO review;
9. inventorship, ownership and disclosure records are complete; and
10. institutional reviewers determine that the likely useful scope justifies filing cost and disclosure risk.

Failure of the evidence gate normally leads to “retain as a strong semester prototype, engineering study or publication candidate; reduce/close patent priority.” It must not lead to a weak filing merely to preserve the original patent aspiration.

Even after all gates pass, the correct language is “eligible for counsel-led file/no-file evaluation,” not “patentable,” “approved for filing,” or “likely to be granted.”
