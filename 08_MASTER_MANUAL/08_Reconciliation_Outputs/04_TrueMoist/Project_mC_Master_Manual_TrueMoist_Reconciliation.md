# Chapter 08 — TrueMoist Architecture, Evidence and Research Position

## 08.1 Controlling TrueMoist identity

TrueMoist is a bounded local sensor-compensation demonstrator for a low-cost analog capacitive soil-moisture probe. Its frozen identity combines:

- one genuine analog capacitive moisture probe used for the final dataset, with a second nominally identical unit used for spare and unit-variation characterization;
- measured soil temperature from a nearby waterproof DS18B20;
- a **known controlled salinity-treatment code** assigned from prepared-batch metadata;
- one homogeneous soil and one controlled preparation method;
- local acquisition and local fixed-coefficient inference on an ESP32-class controller;
- USB serial CSV output;
- gravimetric dry-basis water content as physical reference;
- an authoritative 72-independent-run experimental design.

The frozen identity is not a live-EC meter, irrigation controller, pump controller, cloud platform, remote-farm telemetry system, multi-soil universal model, custom multi-frequency probe, TinyML demonstration, online-adaptive learner or generic precision-agriculture platform.

TrueMoist remains a legitimate retained Phase 3C concept. The attached record does not establish it as the final semester selection, a rejected concept, or a teacher-approved implementation.

**Evidence status:** current frozen architecture and current portfolio status.  
**Source basis:** `03_CONCEPT_PORTFOLIO/TrueMoist/01_Final_Architecture/Phase3C_TrueMoist_Architecture_Report_Final.md` (**TIER_1**); `03_CONCEPT_PORTFOLIO/TrueMoist/01_Final_Architecture/Phase3C_TrueMoist_Memory_Final.md` (**TIER_1**); Decision Register v1.2 (**TIER_1**); validated Part I comparative state.

## 08.2 Bounded project objective

The semester objective is to determine whether local compensation can make an inexpensive analog capacitive probe more trustworthy under controlled one-soil disturbances. The intended comparison is between:

1. an uncompensated estimate derived from the raw probe response; and
2. a locally computed compensated estimate that also uses measured temperature and the known salinity-treatment code.

Both estimates must be compared with the same gravimetric reference on held-out physical samples. The objective is not to prove universal soil-moisture accuracy, cross-soil transfer, long-term field reliability, live soil chemistry, autonomous irrigation or patentability.

The project’s value may be engineering, metrological, demonstrational and publication-oriented even if the ordinary frozen architecture remains patent-weak.

**Evidence status:** current objective and bounded engineering hypothesis; no physical performance result is established.  
**Source basis:** final architecture (**TIER_1**); final SOP (**TIER_1**).

## 08.4 Authoritative 72-run experimental architecture

The 72-run structure is:

```text
4 moisture conditions
× 3 temperature conditions
× 2 controlled salinity treatments
× 3 independent physical replicates
= 72 independent physical condition runs
```

### Meaning of a run

A run is one separately prepared or independently reset physical soil condition. Sixty-four rapid ADC samples, repeated electronic reads or multiple log lines from one undisturbed container are not independent runs. They are repeated electronic observations that must be aggregated into one physical record.

### Factor status

| Factor | Controlling position |
|---|---|
| Moisture | Four dry-basis target regions spanning the safe usable range identified in pilot work; exact percentages are not frozen before soil field capacity and probe response are known |
| Temperature | The architecture recommends 20 °C, 30 °C and 40 °C, with 20/28/36 °C as a documented fallback if 40 °C cannot be maintained safely and uniformly; actual soil temperature must be recorded |
| Salinity | S0 = no deliberately added salt; S1 = a pilot-selected NaCl dose that produces a repeatable, non-saturating effect; exact S1 is frozen after the Week-2 pilot and recorded as grams NaCl per kilogram of oven-dry-equivalent soil |
| Replicate | Three independently prepared or independently reset physical samples per factor combination |

### Configuration and split controls

- Replicates 1 and 2 are designated for training and model selection.
- Replicate 3 is designated as an untouched final physical test set.
- Grouped cross-validation must operate by physical batch within training data.
- Rapid readings from one batch must never be divided between training and test.
- Probe depth, angle, sensor separation, container position, insertion path, dry-soil mass, water addition, mixing, equilibration and compaction are controlled through a fixed jig and procedure.
- Batch, replicate, probe, preparation and acquisition identifiers are logged.

### Evidence status

The attached sources establish the **authoritative experimental design** and planning controls. They do **not** prove that all 72 physical runs were collected, that model fitting occurred, or that held-out validation passed. No completed dataset, measured RMSE/MAE table, physical run ledger or validated final coefficient set is present in the packet.

The packet does not freeze a complete run-order randomization schedule. Run independence is required, and randomization remains an implementation control that must be preregistered rather than inferred.

**Residual uncertainty:** exact moisture targets, exact S1 dose, achievable temperature conditions, run order, throughput and successful completion remain open until pilot and execution evidence exists.

**Source basis:** final architecture §§15–19 (**TIER_1**); final memory (**TIER_1**); final SOP dataset and leakage requirements (**TIER_1**).

## 08.5 Measurement and acquisition architecture

The measurement chain uses a short, deliberate analog path. The primary design places the probe’s analog output through a 1 kΩ series resistor to the ADC input, with a 100 nF capacitor to ground. The ADS1115 is locally decoupled with 100 nF ceramic and 10 µF bulk capacitance. The final analog path is intended to move from breadboard characterization to soldered perfboard by the end of Week 2.

Per physical record, the architecture proposes:

1. wait for soil and temperature equilibration;
2. read the DS18B20;
3. discard the first ADC conversion after a channel or configuration change;
4. acquire 64 ADC observations at the specified ADS1115 data rate;
5. apply median-based rejection or a three-median-absolute-deviation rule;
6. compute the accepted mean and supporting dispersion values;
7. evaluate the uncompensated and compensated estimates;
8. log raw values, estimates, metadata and quality flags.

The 64 electronic observations are not separate training samples.

The ADS1115 is not authoritative merely because of nominal bit depth. It is included to reduce acquisition uncertainty at low cost and is subject to a Week-1 evidence gate against calibrated ESP32 ADC1. The comparison must examine short-term variation, drift, reinsertion repeatability and pilot calibration error.

**Evidence status:** designed acquisition procedure; no completed ADC comparison is attached.  
**Source basis:** final architecture §§11 and 13 (**TIER_1**); final memory (**TIER_1**).

## 08.6 Temperature-measurement role

The DS18B20 provides measured soil temperature as a compensation input and experimental factor. It is placed:

- at the same depth as the active moisture-sensing region;
- approximately 20–30 mm from the moisture probe;
- without touching the probe board;
- in a fixed jig with cable strain relief.

Ambient temperature may be recorded for context but is not a substitute for soil temperature. The design requests 12-bit conversion, three readings with a median value, error-code checks and temperature recording at the start and end of the measurement interval.

Temperature is a measured input; it does not by itself establish accurate temperature compensation. Sensor accuracy, spatial gradients, equilibration and the held-out effect of including temperature remain physical validation questions.

**Evidence status:** current design and proposed procedure; no completed temperature calibration or accuracy result.  
**Source basis:** final architecture §9 (**TIER_1**).

## 08.7 Controlled salinity-treatment coding

The salinity variable is controlled experimental metadata, not a live chemical measurement.

For each prepared batch:

- the operator assigns S0 or S1 according to the known preparation;
- S0 means no deliberately added salt;
- S1 means the pilot-selected NaCl treatment;
- the actual treatment is recorded as grams of NaCl per kilogram of oven-dry-equivalent soil;
- the treatment code and dose are logged with the batch and supplied to offline model fitting and local fixed-coefficient inference.

The code does not measure bulk-soil EC, apparent EC, pore-water EC, solution conductivity or universal soil chemistry. It does not autonomously infer salinity. The valid statement is limited to compensation within the tested soil, preparation, treatment and range.

The future operational meaning of the code outside a controlled prepared-batch experiment is not established. A synthesis explicitly preserves the question of whether the code remains laboratory metadata or would later be supplied by a user. Chapter 08 must not silently convert this unresolved deployment issue into a live sensor capability.

**Evidence status:** current controlled-treatment design; exact S1 dose and operational generalization unresolved.  
**Source basis:** final architecture §10 (**TIER_1**); final memory (**TIER_1**); valid patent syntheses (**TIER_2**, unresolved deployment question).

## 08.8 Soil, moisture-reference and preparation controls

### Soil boundary

The design is restricted to one homogeneous soil and one controlled preparation method. It does not support cross-soil transfer, farm-wide generalization or a universal calibration.

### Gravimetric reference

The primary reference is dry-basis gravimetric water content:

```text
GWC = (wet soil mass - dry soil mass) / dry soil mass
```

The planned procedure is:

1. label container and sample;
2. tare the dry container;
3. record wet soil plus container mass;
4. dry at 105 ± 5 °C for at least 24 hours;
5. cool in a dry covered environment;
6. weigh;
7. return to the oven for 1–2 hours;
8. reweigh;
9. accept constant mass only under the chosen scale-based tolerance;
10. calculate dry-basis GWC.

A 0.01 g scale is preferred. A 0.1 g scale may be usable only with sufficiently large samples and an uncertainty calculation. Volumetric water content must not be reported unless bulk density or a controlled known-volume core is independently measured.

### Preparation and rig controls

The architecture proposes 300–500 g soil in a 500–1000 mL nonconductive container, fixed sensor geometry, fixed dry-soil mass, water added by mass, fixed mixing and sealed equilibration, and a repeatable compaction method. Deviations must be recorded rather than silently corrected.

The oven, scale, constant-mass procedure, packing repeatability and sample throughput are entry gates. If credible gravimetric reference access is unavailable, the architecture becomes conditional and final accuracy claims are prohibited.

**Evidence status:** planned reference and preparation architecture; facility access and completed reference results are not proved.  
**Source basis:** final architecture §§16–17 (**TIER_1**); final memory (**TIER_1**).

## 08.9 Calibration and validation design

Training is performed offline in Python. The controlling model ladder is:

- Model 0: raw/uncompensated univariate calibration;
- Model 1: multivariate linear regression;
- Model 2: ridge-regularized second-order candidate.

Replicates 1 and 2 are used for training and model selection. Replicate 3 is held untouched for the final physical test. Model selection must be grouped by physical batch and must not leak rapid readings or duplicate states across the boundary.

Model 2 is selected only if it improves held-out RMSE over Model 1 by at least 5%, avoids unstable coefficients, preserves monotonic behavior over the tested moisture range, does not materially worsen either salinity subset and produces numerically equivalent Python and MCU output. Otherwise Model 1 is deployed.

After model selection, coefficients are fixed and evaluated locally. Online coefficient updates, recursive self-learning and runtime adaptation are excluded from the MVP.

**Evidence status:** planned calibration and validation design; no fitted model, selected coefficient set or held-out result is attached.  
**Source basis:** final architecture §§14–15 (**TIER_1**); final memory (**TIER_1**).

## 08.10 Uncompensated baseline

The controlling uncompensated baseline is a univariate mapping from raw probe response to estimated dry-basis gravimetric water content:

```text
GWC_hat_raw = a0 + a1 × raw
```

The baseline is necessary because the project’s engineering question is not whether a compensated model can fit data in isolation. It is whether compensation provides held-out improvement over the same probe’s uncompensated estimate against the same physical reference.

The source packet provides the baseline form and proposed comparison method but no fitted coefficients or measured baseline performance.

**Evidence status:** current model specification; performance unavailable.  
**Source basis:** final architecture §14.1 (**TIER_1**).

## 08.11 Compensated method

The first compensated comparison is the multivariate linear baseline:

```text
GWC_hat_linear =
b0 + b1×raw + b2×temperature + b3×salinity_code
```

The final candidate is a ridge-regularized second-order regression:

```text
GWC_hat_comp =
c0
+ c1×raw
+ c2×T
+ c3×S
+ c4×raw²
+ c5×T²
+ c6×raw×T
+ c7×raw×S
+ c8×T×S
```

Terms may be removed if unstable or if they add no held-out benefit. The controlling architecture does not authorize adding a neural network, random forest, online learner, generic TinyML model or undisclosed equation.

The exact coefficients, feature scaling, fitted residuals, calibration curves and error reductions are unavailable because no completed model-fitting result is attached.

**Evidence status:** exact supported model families and equations; no completed model.  
**Source basis:** final architecture §14 (**TIER_1**).

## 08.13 Hardware, firmware and local-output architecture

- ADS1115 primary ADC, comparison-gated;
- genuine analog capacitive probe;
- waterproof DS18B20;
- filtered, decoupled and eventually soldered analog path;

## 08.24 Future validity, transfer or recalibration hypotheses

The strongest future direction supported by the syntheses is not part of the frozen semester MVP. Its working elements are:

1. bind donor calibration and provenance to a probe, probe class and measurement setup;
2. obtain sparse trusted physical responses when a commodity probe is replaced or remounted;
3. derive a bounded transfer mapping;
4. challenge the mapping under controlled physical perturbations;
5. use residual behavior to distinguish unit bias from moisture change, temperature/salinity disturbance, packing/remount error, contamination, electronic offset or degradation;
6. accept transferred coefficients only within a physically validated transfer state;
7. block contaminated reference events from updating calibration;
8. abstain or use a safe fallback when validity is not established.

This is a **candidate mechanism** and **research hypothesis**, not an invention. Broad components are crowded. The differentiating question is whether the combined physical sequence produces a non-additive, materially superior effect over simple baselines such as coefficient reuse, one-point or two-point affine transfer, full per-probe calibration, simple range checks and generic statistical gates.

A two-probe, one-soil pilot can only test feasibility. The syntheses state that credible later evidence would require several probes, preferably multiple manufacturing lots, repeated remounting, packing and contamination challenges, ageing or drift proxies, independent test groups and claim-specific ablations. A passing Level-1 72-run result would not prove Level-2 transfer or validity.

No multi-soil transfer, event-triggered recalibration, lifecycle fingerprint, generic confidence gate or dual-probe health feature should be elevated to the frozen architecture.

**Source basis:** both valid syntheses (**TIER_2**).
