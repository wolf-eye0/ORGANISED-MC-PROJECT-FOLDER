# VibeGuard Architecture: A Low-Cost, DSP-First Framework for Reliable On-Device Vibration Anomaly Detection

## Architectural Investigation and Comparative Analysis

The objective of this investigation is to determine the most appropriate engineering architecture for the VibeGuard prototype, balancing technical validity, cost-effectiveness, and student-buildability within a strict budget and timeline. The analysis begins by evaluating candidate system-level approaches against the project's core constraints, including the directive to prioritize robustness to environmental noise and to treat classical Digital Signal Processing (DSP) as the primary baseline. The architectural choices are constrained by the need to operate on-device without cloud dependency, align with a mixed-skill student team, and remain within a ₹3000–₹5000 INR budget. The engineering philosophy prioritizes the validation of the core hypothesis—the ability to detect abnormal vibration locally—over achieving the sophistication of a complete industrial platform [[13](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12609400/), [36](https://zbotic.in/iot-predictive-maintenance-vibration-sensors-on-esp32/?srsltid=AfmBOoqEDUylLB2FfE8n4KqcLjvznNgxrLurRXtJz9rtKcelcIZQfBru)].

Initial consideration was given to various sensing technologies, including piezoelectric vibration sensors and industrial transducers. However, these were quickly dismissed due to their significantly higher cost, which would consume a disproportionate portion of the project budget [[74](https://m.indiamart.com/proddetail/vibration-monitoring-sensor-2857197295388.html), [75](https://m.indiamart.com/proddetail/mems-accelerometer-3995166830.html)]. For instance, MEMS accelerometers designed for industrial vibration monitoring can cost upwards of ₹50,000, while USB-based units can reach ₹140,000, making them entirely unfeasible for a student project [[74](https://m.indiamart.com/proddetail/vibration-monitoring-sensor-2857197295388.html), [76](https://m.indiamart.com/proddetail/usb-accelerometer-sensor-21700550488.html)]. This constraint firmly establishes MEMS accelerometry as the only viable path forward. The subsequent analysis therefore focuses exclusively on single-sensor architectures based on low-cost MEMS accelerometers, as multi-sensor arrays introduce significant complexity in signal synchronization, data fusion, and mechanical mounting that exceeds the scope and resources of the project [[30](https://github.com/topics/vibration-sensor?l=c%2B%2B&o=desc&s=forks)]. The architecture will be purely edge-based, processing all data locally on a microcontroller to meet the requirement for standalone operation [[54](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393360721_Development_of_a_Real-Time_Vibration_Monitoring_System_for_Drum_Tester_Machines_Using_ADXL345_Sensor_and_ESP32_Microcontroller)].

Within the domain of MEMS accelerometers, two primary candidates emerged from the literature for detailed comparative analysis: the ADXL345 and the MPU-6050 [[2](https://zbotic.in/accelerometer-buying-guide-india-adxl345-vs-mpu6050-vs-lis3dh/?srsltid=AfmBOornG9CGCSuPazWjSWuelG2QMkCN4izXB6jlDCg2-1z0n6f7mFR-), [56](https://zbotic.in/accelerometer-buying-guide-india-adxl345-vs-mpu6050-vs-lis3dh/?srsltid=AfmBOorurJOCEH4OIWeAsyOHq7_KwRyceLdd2p4W0e6Rg8usLu8SSOzD)]. The MPU-6050 is a 6-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) sensor combining a 3-axis accelerometer and a 3-axis gyroscope, often marketed as a cost-effective IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit) [[95](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/accelerometer), [116](https://github.com/derdoktor667/ESP32_MPU6050)]. Its price point is highly attractive, frequently ranging from ₹80 to ₹200 in India, making it one of the cheapest options available [[4](https://robu.in/product/mpu-6050-gyro-sensor-2-accelerometer/), [73](https://www.ktron.in/product/adxl345-triple-axis-accelerometer-module/?srsltid=AfmBOoqS-gkWu5QpT7WV6eL2ORY-X2DSbfneFX_EeegxpiP4wnk0jncr)]. However, its inclusion of a gyroscope introduces a layer of complexity that may not be necessary and could even be detrimental for this specific application. Gyroscopes measure angular velocity and are susceptible to drift and temperature-related errors, which could complicate the interpretation of linear vibration data and introduce noise into the system [[193](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/analog-dialogue/raqs/raq-issue-144.html)]. One study noted that while the MPU6050 was recommended for stability in some mechanical vibration monitoring contexts, another found the ADXL345 to be superior for detecting faulty conditions, underscoring the nuanced nature of the trade-offs [[113](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356622307_Performance_evaluation_of_accelerometers_ADXL345_and_MPU6050_exposed_to_random_vibrational_input), [146](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/16/6015)].

The ADXL345, by contrast, is a dedicated 3-axis digital accelerometer. While slightly more expensive, typically priced between ₹150 and ₹350, its focused functionality offers distinct advantages for this project [[2](https://zbotic.in/accelerometer-buying-guide-india-adxl345-vs-mpu6050-vs-lis3dh/?srsltid=AfmBOornG9CGCSuPazWjSWuelG2QMkCN4izXB6jlDCg2-1z0n6f7mFR-), [23](https://www.mathaelectronics.com/product/adxl345/)]. As a simpler device, it is less susceptible to certain types of electronic interference and has fewer sources of potential error compared to the more complex MPU-6050 [[57](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/adxl345-vs-mpu6050-vs-bno055/1218136)]. It features a high resolution of 13-bit measurements across selectable ranges from ±2g to ±16g, providing sufficient sensitivity for detecting the target anomalies [[26](https://www.tradeindia.com/products/adxl345-sensor-module-4416569.html), [29](https://techdelivers.com/adxl345-triple-axis-accelerometer-board?tag=sensor&srsltid=AfmBOopOAnbIZj2VyseKSpVodzqkKxqle4dtjtjITKYufNsDOETCDTSq)]. Crucially, its widespread use in academic and hobbyist projects means there is a vast body of documentation, tutorials, and pre-existing libraries for interfacing with platforms like Arduino and ESP32, which significantly de-risks the implementation for a student team [[38](https://www.bettlink.com/blog/adxl345-guide), [39](https://pcbsync.com/adxl345-arduino/), [40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XSzRwJNJ4u8)]. Numerous published works have successfully employed the ADXL345 for vibration analysis, including real-time motor health monitoring and drum tester machine monitoring, confirming its suitability and reliability for such tasks [[54](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393360721_Development_of_a_Real-Time_Vibration_Monitoring_System_for_Drum_Tester_Machines_Using_ADXL345_Sensor_and_ESP32_Microcontroller), [55](https://www.ijprems.com/ijprems-paper/vibration-based-motor-health-monitoring-system-using-esp32-and-adxl345), [93](https://in.linkedin.com/in/abhranil-sarkar)]. Given the project's explicit goal of distinguishing normal vibration from deliberate abnormalities like imbalance or misalignment, the cleaner, more direct measurement provided by the ADXL345's accelerometer array makes it the more prudent and less risky choice. The decision favors a component whose behavior is well-understood and whose ecosystem supports rapid development over one that offers extra features at the cost of increased complexity and potential ambiguity.

Based on this analysis, the selected architecture will be a single-node system centered around the ADXL345 MEMS accelerometer. This choice directly addresses the need for a robust, cost-effective, and implementable solution. The focus remains on leveraging the sensor's ability to accurately capture linear vibration data, which is the core requirement for validating the engineering hypothesis. The architecture will not incorporate advanced features like gyroscopic data fusion or complex multi-modal sensing, as these are outside the project's defined scope and budget. The emphasis will instead be on creating a reliable physical installation and developing a simple yet effective signal processing pipeline to interpret the accelerometer's output.

## Sensor Technology Evaluation and Selection

The selection of the vibration sensor is arguably the most critical decision in the VibeGuard architecture, as it forms the foundation of the entire measurement system. The chosen technology must be capable of capturing the target mechanical phenomena—namely, lower-frequency anomalies such as imbalance and misalignment—while remaining compatible with the project's stringent budget of ₹3000–₹5000 and the skill level of a student team. The investigation confirms that industrial-grade piezoelectric accelerometers or specialized high-performance MEMS sensors are financially inaccessible, with prices starting at tens of thousands of rupees [[74](https://m.indiamart.com/proddetail/vibration-monitoring-sensor-2857197295388.html), [75](https://m.indiamart.com/proddetail/mems-accelerometer-3995166830.html)]. Therefore, the analysis is confined to the class of low-cost MEMS accelerometers readily available through Indian suppliers.

Two primary candidates consistently appear in the provided materials for educational and low-cost IoT projects: the ADXL345 and the MPU-6050 [[2](https://zbotic.in/accelerometer-buying-guide-india-adxl345-vs-mpu6050-vs-lis3dh/?srsltid=AfmBOornG9CGCSuPazWjSWuelG2QMkCN4izXB6jlDCg2-1z0n6f7mFR-), [56](https://zbotic.in/accelerometer-buying-guide-india-adxl345-vs-mpu6050-vs-lis3dh/?srsltid=AfmBOorurJOCEH4OIWeAsyOHq7_KwRyceLdd2p4W0e6Rg8usLu8SSOzD), [152](https://zbotic.in/accelerometer-buying-guide-india-adxl345-vs-mpu6050-vs-lis3dh/?srsltid=AfmBOoq0TWhef4pbYfpD7vvFdVrnWSZLzOjtf64Z-Aa5UbwNDR7LLuKc)]. The MPU-6050 is a popular choice among hobbyists due to its extremely low cost, often available for as little as ₹80-₹200, and its integration of both an accelerometer and a gyroscope onto a single chip [[4](https://robu.in/product/mpu-6050-gyro-sensor-2-accelerometer/), [73](https://www.ktron.in/product/adxl345-triple-axis-accelerometer-module/?srsltid=AfmBOoqS-gkWu5QpT7WV6eL2ORY-X2DSbfneFX_EeegxpiP4wnk0jncr)]. While this combination is powerful for applications like drone stabilization or gesture recognition, it introduces elements that are not directly relevant to the core task of linear vibration analysis [[95](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/accelerometer)]. The gyroscope measures angular velocity, which is prone to issues like bias drift and temperature sensitivity, potentially complicating the signal processing chain and introducing unwanted artifacts when analyzing linear acceleration data [[193](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/analog-dialogue/raqs/raq-issue-144.html)]. Furthermore, studies comparing the two devices for vibration monitoring have produced mixed results; one paper recommended the MPU6050 for its stability, while another concluded the ADXL345 was better at detecting faulty conditions, indicating that the "better" sensor can depend heavily on the specific application and implementation [[113](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356622307_Performance_evaluation_of_accelerometers_ADXL345_and_MPU6050_exposed_to_random_vibrational_input), [146](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/16/6015)].

The ADXL345, while generally priced slightly higher (typically ₹150–₹350), is a dedicated 3-axis digital accelerometer [[2](https://zbotic.in/accelerometer-buying-guide-india-adxl345-vs-mpu6050-vs-lis3dh/?srsltid=AfmBOornG9CGCSuPazWjSWuelG2QMkCN4izXB6jlDCg2-1z0n6f7mFR-), [23](https://www.mathaelectronics.com/product/adxl345/)]. Its specialization for measuring linear acceleration makes it a more straightforward and direct tool for the VibeGuard's purpose. It boasts a high-resolution 13-bit measurement capability and allows for selectable full-scale ranges from ±2g to ±16g, offering flexibility for different vibration amplitudes [[26](https://www.tradeindia.com/products/adxl345-sensor-module-4416569.html), [29](https://techdelivers.com/adxl345-triple-axis-accelerometer-board?tag=sensor&srsltid=AfmBOopOAnbIZj2VyseKSpVodzqkKxqle4dtjtjITKYufNsDOETCDTSq)]. Critically, the ADXL345 benefits from extensive community support and a wealth of existing projects, with numerous guides detailing its use with microcontrollers like the ESP32 and Arduino [[38](https://www.bettlink.com/blog/adxl345-guide), [39](https://pcbsync.com/adxl345-arduino/), [40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XSzRwJNJ4u8)]. This rich ecosystem of tutorials, code examples, and troubleshooting advice significantly lowers the barrier to entry for students and reduces the risk of implementation failure. Multiple research papers and student projects have successfully used the ADXL345 for real-time vibration monitoring and fault detection, demonstrating its proven efficacy in similar contexts [[54](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393360721_Development_of_a_Real-Time_Vibration_Monitoring_System_for_Drum_Tester_Machines_Using_ADXL345_Sensor_and_ESP32_Microcontroller), [55](https://www.ijprems.com/ijprems-paper/vibration-based-motor-health-monitoring-system-using-esp32-and-adxl345), [93](https://in.linkedin.com/in/abhranil-sarkar)].

Regarding frequency response, the user's directive specifies a focus on lower-frequency anomalies like imbalance and misalignment, rather than high-frequency bearing faults [[36](https://zbotic.in/iot-predictive-maintenance-vibration-sensors-on-esp32/?srsltid=AfmBOoqEDUylLB2FfE8n4KqcLjvznNgxrLurRXtJz9rtKcelcIZQfBru)]. The ADXL345 datasheet indicates an output data rate up to 3200Hz, theoretically allowing it to capture frequencies up to 1600Hz [[28](https://www.electronicscomp.com/adxl345-triple-axis-linear-accelerometer), [187](https://forums.adafruit.com/viewtopic.php?t=183954)]. While this bandwidth is more than sufficient for the targeted faults, it also ensures that the architecture is scalable. If future iterations of the project wish to explore more subtle anomalies, the sensor is technically capable of doing so. The project's scope explicitly avoids the need for the ultra-low noise densities (e.g., <100 µg/√Hz) characteristic of high-end industrial sensors, making the ADXL345's typical noise density of around 290 µg/√Hz acceptable for this educational prototype [[115](https://www.scribd.com/document/344880140/ADXL345)]. The primary engineering challenge identified in the literature for any MEMS-based system is not raw sensor performance but rather the management of environmental noise and ensuring proper mechanical coupling, challenges that are addressed through architectural design rather than sensor selection alone [[32](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43926-025-00142-4), [79](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/6287639/6514899/10752530.pdf)].

Ultimately, the decision rests on a balance of cost, simplicity, and proven reliability. The minor additional cost of the ADXL345 is justified by its functional purity, reduced implementation risk, and the extensive support network surrounding it. These factors align perfectly with the project's goals of being an educational, reproducible, and reliable prototype. The sensor's specifications are more than adequate for detecting the deliberately introduced abnormal conditions of interest. Therefore, the **ADXL345** is the unequivocally recommended sensing technology for the VibeGuard architecture. Its selection represents the optimal compromise between performance requirements, budgetary constraints, and the practical realities of student-led development.

## Embedded Platform and Electrical Architecture

The selection of the embedded processing platform is a pivotal decision that dictates the system's computational capabilities, memory resources, peripheral support, power consumption, and overall development environment. For the VibeGuard project, the ideal platform must offer a compelling balance of low cost, sufficient performance for the required signal processing tasks, and ease of use for a student team with mixed expertise. The analysis of candidate platforms—primarily the ESP32 family, STM32 series, and Arduino-class MCUs—converges on the ESP32 as the most suitable choice.

The ESP32 microcontroller stands out due to its exceptional combination of features and affordability. Development boards featuring the ESP32 are widely available in India for under ₹500, with prices often cited around ₹350–₹554 [[78](https://zbotic.in/product/esp32-programmer-board/?srsltid=AfmBOoq4ml9135awfnwdOswCOdeO5cNO1P1ReNbnsn2xPUcf2vy9NTtQ), [150](https://aiiottech.in/product-category/development-boards/)]. This low cost fits comfortably within the project's tight budget. More importantly, the ESP32 integrates a dual-core processor, substantial RAM, and built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth connectivity [[90](https://blog.huebits.in/top-10-trending-industrial-iot-projects-for-2025-with-source-code/), [207](https://www.electropi.in/esp32-development-board-with-wifi-bluetooth-india)]. The dual-core architecture can be leveraged to run real-time tasks, such as continuous data acquisition, on one core while the other core handles more computationally intensive tasks like FFT analysis or managing communication protocols. The integrated wireless capabilities are a significant advantage, providing a clear pathway for future scalability beyond the minimum demonstrable prototype, which explicitly requires a standalone device [[9](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?t=5038)]. In contrast, platforms like the STM32, while powerful, often come at a higher price point (with basic boards costing around ₹1530 [[151](https://hubtronics.in/microcontroller-development-boards?page=3&srsltid=AfmBOooZpNMxEea-YdxjbNMA9d8pexK1fvoxUiD_RMKJszaAuQzm4aGj)]) and may require a more complex toolchain for firmware development, presenting a steeper learning curve for beginners [[206](https://probots.co.in/diy-electronic-modules/development-boards-single-board-computers-raspberry-pi/stm32-development-board.html)]. Similarly, classic Arduino boards lack native wireless, and while the RP2040 (used in the Pico) is very low-cost (~₹200-265 [[204](https://hubtronics.in/rp2040-zero-mcu-board?srsltid=AfmBOoqlx7blx-_CIZV32FBBDUx44akt9O4ucqbbKizL7XkqBc8SJwqD), [205](https://www.rees52.in/raspberry-pi-pico-microcontroller-development-board-with-versatile-board-built-using-rp2040-chip-8668555.html)]), adding wireless functionality would necessitate purchasing and integrating a separate module, increasing both cost and complexity [[149](https://hubtronics.in/ultimate-pico-rp2040-dev-board?srsltid=AfmBOoqxElPmX3xQWdmwkfYtMK02ddMqd2bz1hgtlYjtXq9UPk9Q9xva), [176](https://makerbazar.in/products/arduino-nano-rp2040-connect-abx00052?srsltid=AfmBOopzxzVvDimkd0IquB8JHEHHBqnirRF-0G7eJTmqHRxAX5sqlH8z)].

The ESP32's strength is further amplified by its mature software ecosystem. It is fully supported by the Arduino IDE, which hosts a library repository of over 9,000 entries [[118](https://www.arduinolibraries.info/libraries)]. This vast collection includes optimized libraries for communicating with the ADXL345 sensor (e.g., Adafruit's driver) and for performing Fast Fourier Transforms (e.g., KISS FFT) [[10](https://blog.adafruit.com/2026/04/28/the-kiss-fft-arduino-esp32-library/), [38](https://www.bettlink.com/blog/adxl345-guide)]. The availability of these pre-built, tested components dramatically accelerates development and minimizes debugging time for the student team. The extensive online community provides a wealth of tutorials, forums, and example code, which is invaluable for troubleshooting common issues related to sensor interfacing, wireless communication, or real-time programming [[9](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?t=5038), [88](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1006568440013226/posts/1808533646483364/)]. This accessible environment is perfectly suited to the team's composition, where three members have stronger CS backgrounds and can leverage this ecosystem effectively, while the beginner programmer can work on more manageable firmware tasks using familiar frameworks.

The electrical architecture for the VibeGuard prototype can be kept intentionally simple. Since the selected ADXL345 sensor is a digital device, it communicates with the ESP32 via a standard I2C interface [[114](https://www.sparkfun.com/datasheets/Sensors/Accelerometer/ADXL345.pdf?srsltid=AfmBOoqhS2gEB2Y-FDs6efYQMjpPhJ_E39w16rAO5J5MF0F6mDKxx-mj)]. This requires only four connections: VCC (power), GND (ground), SCL (clock), and SDA (data). Pull-up resistors on the SDA and SCL lines are necessary for proper I2C bus operation, but these are simple passive components. Power regulation is also straightforward; the ESP32 can be powered via USB or a DC-DC converter, and the ADXL345 can operate from either 3.3V or 5V logic levels, which is easily managed by the ESP32's onboard voltage regulator or external circuitry [[5](https://robu.in/product/adxl345-tripple-axis-accelerometer-board/), [23](https://www.mathaelectronics.com/product/adxl345/)]. There is no need for a complex analog front-end or signal conditioning circuit, as the sensor performs the analog-to-digital conversion internally. Status indicators, such as LEDs connected to GPIO pins on the ESP32, can provide visual feedback on the system's state (e.g., "Normal," "Abnormal," "Acquiring Data"), fulfilling part of the minimum demonstrable success criteria in a low-cost manner. The overall electrical design prioritizes reliability and ease of assembly, avoiding unnecessary components to stay within budget and reduce potential points of failure. This minimalist approach ensures that the focus remains on the core signal processing and classification algorithms.

## Signal Processing and Edge Intelligence Strategy

The strategy for processing vibration data and making intelligent decisions on the edge is central to the VibeGuard's engineering hypothesis. The user's directive is clear: classical Digital Signal Processing (DSP) must serve as the baseline due to its maturity, explainability, and low implementation risk. Advanced techniques like TinyML are to be considered only as a potential enhancement if they provide a clear and justifiable benefit that outweighs their increased complexity. This philosophy steers the architecture toward a pragmatic, layered approach that maximizes reliability and student feasibility.

For the signal processing pipeline, a hybrid, tiered methodology is recommended. The first and most fundamental layer involves time-domain analysis. The simplest and most robust feature to calculate is the Root Mean Square (RMS) value of the acceleration signal [[99](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/6287639/9668973/09913462.pdf)]. The RMS value is a direct measure of the overall energy or magnitude of the vibration and is highly effective at detecting gross changes indicative of large, persistent faults like rotor imbalance or structural looseness [[147](https://www.arduinolibraries.info/libraries/mpu6050_vibration-rms), [188](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/The-vibration-data-recorded-using-ADXL345-accelerometer_tbl4_362647463)]. Calculating the RMS value is computationally inexpensive and can be performed continuously on the ESP32, providing a constant stream of data representing the machine's vibrational energy. Alongside RMS, calculating the standard deviation or variance of the signal can also provide useful information about the signal's fluctuation and help identify periods of instability [[99](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/6287639/9668973/09913462.pdf)].

To move beyond simple energy detection and begin identifying specific fault signatures, a second layer involving frequency-domain analysis is necessary. The Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) is the canonical tool for converting a time-domain signal into its constituent frequencies [[10](https://blog.adafruit.com/2026/04/28/the-kiss-fft-arduino-esp32-library/)]. By applying an FFT to a window of acquired data, the system can generate a frequency spectrum that reveals dominant peaks corresponding to the machine's rotational speed and its harmonics. For instance, a significant peak at the first harmonic of the rotational frequency is a classic indicator of mass imbalance [[99](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/6287639/9668973/09913462.pdf)]. The availability of efficient C-based FFT libraries like KISS FFT, which are designed to integrate seamlessly with the Arduino framework on the ESP32, makes this implementation feasible [[10](https://blog.adafruit.com/2026/04/28/the-kiss-fft-arduino-esp32-library/), [11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mgh2WblO5_c)]. However, performing a full FFT on every sample is computationally demanding and would consume too much of the ESP32's processing resources. A more practical approach is to perform the FFT intermittently. The system can rely on the fast RMS calculation for real-time monitoring. Only when the RMS value crosses a predefined threshold—an initial indicator of an anomaly—would the system trigger a more resource-intensive process: acquiring a larger buffer of data and executing an FFT on that buffer to characterize the nature of the fault.

This leads to the edge intelligence strategy. For the minimum demonstrable prototype, the strongest and most reliable approach is a **threshold-based classifier**. This method aligns perfectly with the project's goals and constraints. During a calibration or "normal condition" phase, the system would continuously monitor the RMS value and establish a baseline. A threshold, perhaps set at a multiple of the standard deviation above the mean normal RMS, would then be defined. Any subsequent RMS reading that exceeds this threshold would be classified as "Abnormal." This approach is deterministic, has near-zero inference latency, requires minimal code, and is completely transparent and explainable—a key advantage over "black box" ML models. It directly satisfies the minimum demonstrable success criteria with maximum engineering integrity.

TinyML, while a powerful emerging technology, introduces a significant increase in implementation risk and complexity that is not warranted for this initial prototype. Research demonstrates that TinyML models can achieve very high accuracy in motor fault detection, sometimes exceeding 96% [[32](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43926-025-00142-4), [33](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16553v1)]. However, realizing this performance requires a non-trivial effort: collecting and meticulously labeling a large dataset of vibration signals under both normal and abnormal operating conditions [[180](https://blog.endaq.com/using-vibration-data-for-high-level-machine-learning)]. It also demands expertise in machine learning frameworks, model training, and optimization techniques like quantization to make the model small and fast enough to run on an MCU [[33](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16553v1)]. Furthermore, studies highlight that even sophisticated ML models can be sensitive to environmental noise, potentially leading to false positives if not carefully designed, a problem the project aims to solve through architectural robustness [[32](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43926-025-00142-4)]. Therefore, while the potential for a future upgrade to a TinyML-based classifier exists, it should be treated as a secondary enhancement. The primary focus for the VibeGuard prototype must remain on building and validating the robust, reliable, and interpretable baseline system using classical DSP and a simple threshold classifier.

## Mechanical Integration and Environmental Noise Mitigation

The mechanical integration of the sensor and the mitigation of environmental noise represent the most significant engineering risks for the VibeGuard prototype. A flawed physical installation can render even the most sophisticated electronics ineffective, as poor vibration coupling will result in a corrupted signal that cannot be corrected by software. The user's directive to prioritize robustness to environmental interference underscores the paramount importance of this subsystem. The architecture must be designed to ensure consistent, repeatable measurements while filtering out extraneous noise from nearby machinery and ambient sources.

The sensor mounting strategy is the first line of defense for ensuring signal fidelity. The choice of mount directly impacts the efficiency of vibration transfer from the machine frame to the sensor die. Common methods include adhesive mounting, magnetic mounting, and rigid bolt mounting. Magnetic mounts offer convenience and repositionability but suffer from poor repeatability and can easily become detached during testing, leading to inconsistent data [[103](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/7361/4427201/11080221.pdf)]. Adhesive mounting, such as with epoxy or industrial-strength glue, provides a more secure bond but can be difficult to remove and may degrade over time. For a prototype intended for repeated experiments and demonstrations, the most robust and reliable method is **rigid bolt mounting**. Securing the sensor package directly to a clean, flat surface on the machine housing with a small screw or bolt ensures maximum and consistent vibration coupling [[103](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/7361/4427201/11080221.pdf)]. This method, while requiring more setup time, guarantees the highest possible fidelity of the measured signal and is essential for achieving repeatable results. The design of a custom printed enclosure can facilitate this by incorporating a tapped hole or a bracket for secure sensor attachment [[86](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/24/9677)].

Once the sensor is physically secured, the architecture must address the challenge of discriminating the target machine's vibration from ambient noise. The project's core hypothesis depends on this ability. Several architectural principles are employed to enhance this discrimination. First, **proximity** is key. Placing the sensor as close as physically possible to the source of the vibration naturally increases the signal-to-noise ratio, as the target signal attenuates less over distance than many ambient noise sources. Second, the **feature extraction strategy** plays a crucial role. By focusing on the Root Mean Square (RMS) value for baseline monitoring, the system prioritizes overall vibrational energy, a property inherent to the machine itself [[99](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/6287639/9668973/09913462.pdf)]. When frequency-domain analysis is triggered, the ability to identify characteristic fault frequencies (e.g., rotational harmonics) provides another layer of discrimination. Machine-specific faults often produce distinct spectral signatures that may occupy different frequency bands than general ambient factory noise, which can be broadband or concentrated in other octaves [[21](https://www.science.gov/topicpages/t/three-component+digital+seismometers), [59](https://thermairsystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/ASHRAE-HANDBOOK-Sound-and-Vibration-Control.pdf)]. HVAC systems, for example, often generate significant noise in octave bands from 250 Hz to 8000 Hz [[59](https://thermairsystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/ASHRAE-HANDBOOK-Sound-and-Vibration-Control.pdf)], whereas a misaligned shaft might produce a strong 1x rotational frequency peak at a much lower frequency. Filtering the signal in these known bands can help isolate the machine's own vibrations.

Finally, the **in-situ baseline establishment** is a critical feature of the proposed architecture. Instead of relying on a generic threshold, the system will measure and store the "Normal" condition parameters (RMS value and spectral signature) while the machine is running under controlled, known-good conditions. This process inherently captures the local background noise level at that specific moment and location. Consequently, the system becomes implicitly calibrated to its immediate environment, improving its ability to detect deviations caused by the deliberately introduced abnormality. This approach acknowledges that absolute vibration levels are meaningless without context; the relative change from a verified normal state is what truly matters. The architecture deliberately avoids proposing complex, multi-node spatial filtering arrays, as their implementation complexity and cost are prohibitive. Instead, it relies on the quality of the single sensor's measurement, the robustness of the physical mounting, and the intelligence of the classification logic to achieve the desired outcome. This simplification is a conscious trade-off to manage risk and ensure the project remains achievable within the specified constraints.

## Final Architecture Recommendation and Implementation Feasibility

This report has systematically investigated and analyzed the engineering architecture for the VibeGuard predictive maintenance prototype, culminating in a definitive recommendation that prioritizes engineering validity, cost-effectiveness, and student-buildability. The final architecture is designed to robustly validate the project's core engineering hypothesis within the strict constraints of a ₹3000–₹5000 budget, a semester-long timeline, and a five-member student team. Every component and strategy has been selected based on evidence from the provided sources, adhering to the principle of choosing the simplest solution that meets the requirements.

The recommended engineering architecture for VibeGuard is as follows:

*   **Sensing Architecture:** A single-axis or triaxial **ADXL345** MEMS accelerometer. This sensor is the optimal choice due to its excellent balance of cost (₹150–₹350), proven reliability in academic vibration monitoring projects, high resolution (13-bit), and implementation simplicity [[2](https://zbotic.in/accelerometer-buying-guide-india-adxl345-vs-mpu6050-vs-lis3dh/?srsltid=AfmBOornG9CGCSuPazWjSWuelG2QMkCN4izXB6jlDCg2-1z0n6f7mFR-), [38](https://www.bettlink.com/blog/adxl345-guide), [54](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393360721_Development_of_a_Real-Time_Vibration_Monitoring_System_for_Drum_Tester_Machines_Using_ADXL345_Sensor_and_ESP32_Microcontroller)].
*   **Processing Architecture:** An **ESP32** microcontroller development board. This platform provides the best combination of affordability (under ₹500), sufficient dual-core processing power, integrated Wi-Fi/Bluetooth for future scalability, and a vast, supportive software ecosystem with ready-made libraries for sensor interfacing and signal processing [[118](https://www.arduinolibraries.info/libraries), [150](https://aiiottech.in/product-category/development-boards/), [207](https://www.electropi.in/esp32-development-board-with-wifi-bluetooth-india)].
*   **Signal Acquisition:** Continuous sampling of the ADXL345's digital output via the I2C interface. The output data rate can be configured up to 3200 Hz, though a lower rate (e.g., >100 Hz) will suffice for detecting the target lower-frequency faults [[28](https://www.electronicscomp.com/adxl345-triple-axis-linear-accelerometer), [187](https://forums.adafruit.com/viewtopic.php?t=183954)].
*   **Signal Processing Pipeline:** A hybrid, tiered approach. The system will continuously calculate the **Root Mean Square (RMS)** value of the vibration signal in the time domain. This provides a low-latency, computationally cheap measure of overall vibrational energy. Upon detection of an anomaly via the RMS threshold, the system will acquire a larger buffer of data and perform a **Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)** to analyze the frequency content and identify characteristic fault signatures [[10](https://blog.adafruit.com/2026/04/28/the-kiss-fft-arduino-esp32-library/), [99](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/6287639/9668973/09913462.pdf)].
*   **Edge Intelligence Strategy:** A **threshold-based classifier**. The system will establish a "Normal" operating envelope during an initial calibration phase. Any subsequent measurement that deviates significantly from this baseline (specifically, an RMS value exceeding a calculated threshold) will be classified as "Abnormal." This method is deterministic, has zero inference latency, and is fully transparent, making it ideal for the minimum demonstrable prototype [[96](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3568671), [99](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/6287639/9668973/09913462.pdf)].
*   **Electrical Architecture:** A minimalist design consisting of the ESP32 board, the ADXL345 sensor, basic pull-up resistors for the I2C bus, and a simple power supply (e.g., USB or a 3.7V Li-ion battery with a voltage regulator). No complex analog front-end is required due to the sensor's digital nature [[114](https://www.sparkfun.com/datasheets/Sensors/Accelerometer/ADXL345.pdf?srsltid=AfmBOoqhS2gEB2Y-FDs6efYQMjpPhJ_E39w16rAO5J5MF0F6mDKxx-mj)].
*   **Mechanical Integration:** The sensor will be **rigidly bolt-mounted** to a clean, flat surface on the machine frame. This is the most critical step for ensuring accurate and repeatable vibration transfer, thereby maximizing signal fidelity and minimizing the impact of loose connections or poor coupling [[103](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/7361/4427201/11080221.pdf)].
