# Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

## Phase 3 — Technical Research & Engineering Landscape Analysis

**Methodology Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Active

---

# Purpose

Phase 3 evaluates the current engineering landscape surrounding the five shortlisted project concepts.

Unlike Phase 2, which focused on intellectual property and patent ecosystems, Phase 3 focuses on engineering knowledge, implementation approaches, technical feasibility, and the current state of research.

The objective is **not** to design the final system.

The objective is to determine whether each concept can realistically be engineered within the project's constraints and to identify the major technical challenges that must be solved.

---

# Objectives

Phase 3 aims to:

- Understand the current engineering state-of-the-art.
- Review existing academic and industrial implementations.
- Identify common engineering approaches.
- Identify major technical challenges.
- Evaluate implementation complexity.
- Assess engineering feasibility within project constraints.
- Identify unresolved engineering problems.
- Compare the engineering maturity of all five concepts.

The outcome should help determine which concepts are realistic candidates for prototype development.

---

# Scope

This phase investigates:

- Engineering literature
- Embedded systems research
- Academic publications
- Industrial implementations
- Open-source projects (where relevant)
- Engineering best practices
- Common algorithms
- Typical embedded architectures (high level)
- Sensor categories
- Communication technologies
- AI techniques (where applicable)
- Embedded security approaches
- Signal processing approaches
- Human-computer interaction considerations
- Calibration methods
- Environmental robustness
- Reliability considerations
- Power management concepts
- Scalability considerations

---

# Out of Scope

Phase 3 shall NOT:

- Select hardware components.
- Produce Bills of Materials.
- Recommend specific microcontrollers.
- Produce electrical schematics.
- Produce PCB designs.
- Produce software architecture.
- Produce firmware architecture.
- Estimate implementation schedules.
- Estimate prototype cost.
- Produce implementation plans.
- Produce coding guidance.
- Produce patent opinions.

These activities belong to later phases.

# Research Questions

For every concept Phase 3 should answer:

## Engineering Landscape

How are similar systems currently engineered?

---

## Technical State-of-the-Art

What technologies currently dominate this area?

---

## Common Engineering Approaches

What implementation strategies are most frequently used?

---

## Research Trends

What topics are receiving increased research attention?

---

## Engineering Challenges

What technical problems remain unresolved?

---

## Complexity Assessment

Which parts appear straightforward?

Which parts appear difficult?

Why?

---

## Embedded Systems Requirements

What embedded capabilities are typically required?

Examples:

- sensing
- communication
- edge intelligence
- low-power operation
- cybersecurity
- storage
- real-time processing

---

## AI Requirements

If AI is involved:

What level of AI is typically required?

Examples:

- classical ML
- anomaly detection
- TinyML
- rule-based systems
- deep learning

---

## Hardware Categories

Without recommending specific components,

identify categories such as:

- vibration sensing
- temperature sensing
- moisture sensing
- secure elements
- communication modules

---

## Software Categories

Identify software domains involved.

Examples:

- embedded firmware
- signal processing
- networking
- edge AI
- cybersecurity
- mobile interfaces
- cloud integration

---

## Reliability Considerations

Identify common causes of engineering failure.

---

## Calibration Requirements

Determine whether calibration represents a major engineering challenge.

---

## Environmental Constraints

Consider:

- noise
- vibration
- temperature
- humidity
- electromagnetic interference

where relevant.

---

## Open Engineering Problems

Identify technical questions that remain unanswered by current literature.

# Success Criteria

Phase 3 is successful when:

- The engineering landscape is clearly understood.
- Major engineering risks are identified.
- Existing engineering approaches are documented.
- Technical uncertainty is reduced.
- All five concepts have comparable engineering analyses.
- The findings support future engineering planning.

# Notes

Phase 3 evaluates engineering knowledge.

It does not design products.

It prepares the project for Phase 4, where detailed engineering planning and prototype architecture begin.

Engineering decisions made after Phase 3 should be evidence-based and supported by the findings documented in the Phase3_Report.
