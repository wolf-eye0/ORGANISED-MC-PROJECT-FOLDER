## 4. Frozen VibeGuard semester definition

### 4.1 Project identity

**Source-derived fact:** VibeGuard is a passive, single-node, locally processed machine-condition monitoring prototype. The semester claim is deliberately narrow: distinguish a controlled normal rig state from a deliberately introduced abnormal-vibration state and show the result locally.

### 4.2 Frozen block definition

```text
Rigid machine/rig mount
        ↓
One ADXL345-class tri-axis accelerometer
        ↓ 3.3 V SPI
ESP32-WROOM-32 or authorized ESP32-S3
        ↓
DC removal and continuous time-domain features
        ↓
RMS + persistence minimum classifier
        ↓ when triggered
FFT and selected band features
        ↓
Optional ordinary Mahalanobis comparison
        ↓
RGB local state + USB logs
```

### 4.3 Included semester functions

- rigid sensor mounting;
- SPI acquisition;
- timestamped raw-axis or reproducible raw-window capture;
- normal-state calibration;
- RMS plus persistence decision baseline;
- triggered FFT and band features;
- optional ordinary Mahalanobis as a comparator, not mandatory novelty;
- RGB states;
- USB logs;
- one guarded motor/fan test rig;
- a safe, repeatable eccentric-mass or equivalent abnormal condition;
- independent sessions and held-out runs;
- resource measurements on the ESP32.

### 4.4 Explicit semester exclusions

- cloud dependency;
- permanent second sensor;
- microphone or multi-sensor source-separation array;
- active vibration cancellation;
- industrial failure prediction;
- remaining-useful-life estimation;
- dense-factory target attribution;
- PIRG implementation;
- patent-performance claims.

### 4.5 Semester success boundary

A successful semester build demonstrates stable acquisition, a reproducible normal condition, a reproducible induced abnormal condition, local classification, USB evidence and measured false-alarm/missed-detection behavior on held-out sessions. It does **not** prove industrial predictive maintenance or mixed-source attribution.

## 5. Frozen OpenBraille semester definition

### 5.1 Project identity

**Source-derived fact:** OpenBraille is an assistive embedded prototype investigating whether one macro-scale six-dot Braille cell can be refreshed using low-cost electromechanical parts.

### 5.2 Frozen block definition

```text
USB/serial character or dot command
        ↓
ESP32 direct six-channel PWM
        ↓
Six SG90-class servos
        ↓
Six eccentric cams and followers
        ↓
Six guided vertical tactile pins
        ↓
One macro-scale six-dot cell
```

### 5.3 Included semester functions

- one macro-scale six-dot cell;
- six independently controlled SG90-class servos;
- one eccentric cam, follower and guided vertical pin per dot;
- FDM frame, guides and cams;
- active servo-driven raising and retraction;
- external regulated servo power;
- USB/serial input;
- Braille pattern lookup;
- per-dot PWM calibration;
- single-dot-first commissioning;
- six-dot pattern demonstration;
- repeatability, power and wear observations;
- local fault logs.

### 5.4 Explicit semester exclusions

- moving roller or dimpled wheel;
- encoder wheel;
- Arduino Mega/RAMPS embosser;
- continuous paper;
- electromagnetic actuator substitution;
- standard-size commercial Braille claim;
- VPD-C7 contact-datum mechanism;
- current-only physical-state verification;
- patent-performance claims.

### 5.5 Important correction

The older architecture report and memory contain a passive-return statement. Decision Register v1.2 and the uncertainty register require physical verification. Until a procured servo proves an adequate passive return under load, the build description must use **active cam-driven retraction**. A servo command or visual motor movement is not evidence that a pin reached the correct tactile height.

### 6.1 Specification table

| Hardware | Current official verification | Semester implication |
|---|---|---|
| ADXL345 | Analog Devices describes it as a small, low-power three-axis accelerometer with digital SPI/I²C interfaces, 13-bit measurement up to ±16 g and output-data-rate settings up to 3,200 Hz. | Suitable for the approved low-frequency imbalance/rig experiment if the actual signal lies within the verified band. ### 6.2 Specification cautions

- Generic breakout boards are not equivalent merely because the sensor name is printed on them.
- The ADXL345 board’s power arrangement, decoupling, axes and output-data-rate behavior must be checked.
- ESP32 development-board pinouts, USB-UART chips and regulator quality vary.
- SG90-class servo quality and calibration can vary substantially between sellers and batches.
- No official data sheet replaces physical acceptance testing of the purchased parts.

## 7. Current Indian market-price research methodology

### 7.1 Method

Prices were checked on **3 August 2026** using:

1. manufacturer sources for technical specifications;
2. established Indian electronics retailers for component listings;
3. more than one retailer for major cost-driving items where feasible;
4. GST normalization where a retailer displayed a pre-tax amount;
5. a separate logistics/contingency line rather than hiding delivery in component prices.

### 7.2 Price interpretation

- **Low:** a currently visible credible listing or a minimal local-material allowance.
- **Expected:** the planning value recommended for teacher-facing budgeting.
- **Conservative:** a higher-quality, fallback, alternate-board or local procurement envelope.
- Retail prices are snapshots, not quotations.
- Delivery is not included unless the retailer explicitly included it.
- ElectronicsComp prices used below were normalized by adding 18% GST when the page displayed an excluding-GST price.
- Marketplace listings without a dependable specification or product identity were not used as the only basis for a major part.
- External patent-research equipment is excluded from semester totals.

## 15. VibeGuard future-research boundary

### 15.1 Hypothesis

The pre-build simulation proposes **PIRG — Paired-Intervention Residual Gate** as a future research hypothesis. In teacher-safe terms, a later experiment would separately observe:

- how the target machine’s vibration changes while interference is controlled; and
- how neighbouring interference changes while the target remains independently normal.

One permanent target-mounted sensor would then attempt to decide whether a later deviation resembles a target change or an interference change. Ambiguous cases would not be forced into a confident fault output.

### 15.2 Strict separation from the semester MVP

- PIRG is unbuilt.
- It is not required for semester success.
- The semester rig should preserve raw data, mount records, timing integrity and contributor history so later research is possible.
- No teacher document should state that single-sensor source attribution has been achieved.
- Exact unpublished model structure, thresholds and claim-oriented wording should remain outside public-facing material.

### 15.3 High-level 6–12-month roadmap

| Stage | Research purpose | Evidence gate |
|---|---|---|
| Months 0–2 | Complete and freeze the semester Level-1 platform; design a safe target/interferer bench | Stable acquisition and independently labelled physical states |
| Months 2–4 | Run paired physical interventions and compare against ordinary RMS, FFT/bands, Mahalanobis and conventional single-sensor classifiers | Improvement must appear on held-out complete sessions, not leaked windows |
| Months 4–6 | Test remounts, speed/load changes and ambiguity/Unknown behavior | Useful decision coverage without hiding missed target faults |
| Months 6–9 | Replicate on field-like or accessible machine arrangements with independent labels | Effect survives more than one artificial arrangement |
| Months 9–12 | Refresh claim-level prior art, document inventorship, obtain institutional IPR and professional filing/no-filing review | File only if evidence, useful scope and FTO review justify it |

### 15.4 Kill conditions

Stop the patent-development route if target and interference changes are not distinguishable from one sensor; results disappear on held-out sessions/remounts; the method only succeeds by abstaining excessively; permanent reference sensors are required; the sensor bandwidth is inadequate; or the surviving scope is only a generic classifier.

## 16. OpenBraille future-research boundary

### 16.1 Hypothesis

The pre-build simulation proposes **VPD-C7 — Verified Pin-Datum Cell** as a future mechanical research hypothesis. In teacher-safe terms, later work may investigate whether the same mechanically registered endpoint that sets the tactile pin’s physical position can also provide direct pin-side endpoint evidence. Servo current would remain separate effort, overload or supply information; it would never independently certify the pin height.

### 16.2 Strict separation from the semester MVP

- VPD-C7 is unbuilt.
- It is not part of the frozen six-servo macro cell.
- It should be developed on a separate post-MVP coupon.
- Current-only verification is prohibited.
- Exact contact geometry, dimensions, thresholds, CAD and comparative results should remain confidential until IPR review.
- The unresolved Indian application and close current patent families must be professionally checked before any filing decision.

### 16.3 High-level 6–12-month roadmap

| Stage | Research purpose | Evidence gate |
|---|---|---|
| Months 0–2 | Complete frozen MVP and obtain official close-prior-art records; create direct displacement ground truth | Stable single-dot motion and a clear search boundary |
| Months 2–4 | Compare calibrated open loop, passive datum, separate direct sensor and dual-purpose datum coupons | Same-datum design must show a distinct physical advantage |
| Months 4–7 | Build practical-scale coupons; test contact force, friction, wear, contamination and wiring | No unacceptable tactile/load or false-endpoint failure |
| Months 7–10 | Blind servo/part replacement, reassembly and lifecycle tests; locked held-out transitions | Result transfers across units/builds with no silent wrong-state acceptance |
| Months 10–12 | Refresh worldwide claims, document inventorship/disclosures, obtain institutional IPR and professional filing/no-filing review | File only if the narrow physical effect remains useful and defensible |

### 16.4 Kill conditions

Stop the patent-development route if a separate sensor performs equivalently; the dual-purpose datum adds friction, wear or contamination failures; practical pitch is infeasible; performance collapses after replacement/reassembly; a silently wrong state occurs in the locked gate; closer prior art is found; or useful scope collapses to exact hobby parts and dimensions.

## 18. Recommended teacher-facing change rationale

### 18.1 Evidence-based rationale

The requested switch should be explained through five semester factors:

1. **Build probability:** VibeGuard has fewer mechanically coupled failure points.
2. **Team fit:** its core work aligns with ESP32, firmware, signal processing, data analysis and embedded validation.
3. **Quantitative validation:** normal and induced-abnormal sessions can be labelled, repeated and held out.
4. **Budget headroom:** its expected and conservative costs leave more recovery room.
5. **Demonstration clarity:** a machine-state change, live local indication and logged features can be shown directly.

### 18.2 Balanced treatment of OpenBraille

OpenBraille should be acknowledged as a meaningful accessibility and mechatronics concept whose zeroth-review work remains valid. The switch is not because OpenBraille is “bad” or because VibeGuard is “patented.” It is because OpenBraille’s semester success depends more strongly on repeated mechanical fabrication, friction control, six-servo power behavior and trustworthy tactile-state validation.

### 18.3 Recommended core sentence for the later request document

> Following the zeroth review, the team completed the planned comparative engineering, budget, validation and future-research assessment. That assessment retained OpenBraille as a valuable accessibility research direction but identified VibeGuard as the lower-risk and better-aligned semester implementation. We therefore request approval to change the semester build to the frozen VibeGuard Level-1 architecture while preserving the OpenBraille work as a documented future project.

This wording requests a build decision without making a patent promise.
