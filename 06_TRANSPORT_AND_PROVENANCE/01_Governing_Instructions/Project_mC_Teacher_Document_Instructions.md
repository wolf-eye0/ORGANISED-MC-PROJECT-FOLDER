# Project mC Teacher-Document Master Instructions

## Purpose

Prepare a consistent evidence and costing foundation for three separate
teacher-facing documents:

1. Project mC Request to Change from OpenBraille to VibeGuard.
2. VibeGuard Semester Implementation and Future Research Roadmap.
3. OpenBraille Semester Architecture and Future Research Reference Dossier.

Do not create all three final documents in the initial response.

The first response must create only:

# Project mC Teacher Documents — Master Evidence, Costing and Decision Basis

The three final documents will be requested sequentially after the master basis
is reviewed.

## Audience

The main audience is the semester-project teacher and, secondarily, the
department or institutional innovation/IPR reviewers.

Use clear professional engineering language. Explain difficult technical
concepts accurately without making the documents sound like patent filings.

Do not assume the teacher has read the patent reports.

## Authority order

Apply this order:

1. Engineering Design Review.
2. Project mC Decision Register v1.2.
3. Approved Phase 3C Portfolio Closure Memo.
4. Concept Evidence Matrix.
5. Uncertainty and Test Register.
6. Final concept-specific Architecture Report.
7. Final concept-specific Memory.
8. Final concept-specific SOP.
9. Final cross-concept adjudications as comparative decision context.
10. Pre-build simulations as future research hypotheses, not proof.
11. Fresh official specifications and current market research.

Where a later simulation proposes a future mechanism, it must not rewrite the
frozen semester architecture.

## Project-change framing

The zeroth-review presentation used OpenBraille because final comparative
engineering and patent-path work was not yet complete.

Do not describe OpenBraille as a mistake, failure or abandoned project.

The teacher-facing explanation should state that:

- OpenBraille remains a valuable accessibility and mechatronics concept.
- The later comparative review found VibeGuard better aligned with the semester
  time, budget, team skills, validation and live-demonstration requirements.
- The requested switch is based primarily on semester execution quality, not a
  promise of patent grant.
- Work already completed on OpenBraille remains useful as documented research
  and a possible future project.

## Frozen VibeGuard semester architecture

Preserve:

- one rigidly mounted ADXL345-class tri-axis accelerometer;
- ESP32-WROOM-32 or ESP32-S3 as authorized;
- SPI acquisition;
- local preprocessing;
- time-domain features;
- RMS plus persistence as the minimum classifier;
- triggered FFT and defined band features;
- ordinary Mahalanobis only as a prospective comparison;
- RGB local status;
- USB logging/interface;
- isolated motor or fan normal-versus-induced-abnormal demonstration;
- no cloud dependency;
- no permanent second deployed sensor;
- no source-separation array;
- no active cancellation;
- no remaining-useful-life promise.

The semester MVP is not represented as a patentable invention.

## Future VibeGuard research boundary

The VibeGuard pre-build simulation proposes a research hypothesis called the
Paired-Intervention Residual Gate, or PIRG.

Treat PIRG as:

- hypothetical;
- unbuilt;
- confidential research planning;
- separate from the semester MVP;
- subject to technical kill conditions and professional patent review.

At teacher-facing level explain only that future work may compare physical
target-machine changes with independently controlled interference changes so
that one target-mounted sensor can attempt to distinguish target faults from
neighbouring-machine vibration.

Do not imply that the mechanism works.

Do not publicly expose unnecessary claim-drafting detail, exact thresholds,
private model parameters or unpublished implementation details.

## Frozen OpenBraille semester architecture

Preserve:

- one macro-scale six-dot cell;
- six SG90-class servos;
- six eccentric cams;
- followers and guided vertical pins;
- ESP32 local direct-PWM control;
- FDM frame, guides and cams;
- external regulated servo power;
- USB or serial character input;
- active servo-driven retraction unless passive return is experimentally
  proven;
- no moving roller;
- no encoder wheel;
- no RAMPS or Arduino-Mega embosser;
- no continuous-paper mechanism;
- no electromagnetic-actuator substitution.

The semester OpenBraille architecture is a prototype and is not represented as
a currently patentable invention.

## Future OpenBraille research boundary

The OpenBraille pre-build simulation proposes a future research hypothesis
called the Verified Pin-Datum Cell, or VPD-C7.

Treat VPD-C7 as:

- hypothetical;
- unbuilt;
- confidential research planning;
- separate from the frozen semester MVP;
- subject to mechanical, lifecycle, prior-art and professional patent gates.

At teacher-facing level explain that future work may investigate whether the
same mechanically registered endpoint that establishes the tactile-pin
position can also provide direct pin-side endpoint evidence, while actuator
current is used only as separate effort or overload information.

Do not state that current alone verifies tactile-pin position.

Do not reveal unnecessary dimensions, unpublished contact designs, thresholds
or filing-oriented claim language.

## Current-cost research requirements

Use fresh web research during the master-basis stage.

For all semester hardware:

- prioritize reputable Indian electronics retailers, authorized distributors,
  manufacturer stores and established marketplaces;
- record the price-check date;
- cite the actual sources;
- use at least two sources for major cost-driving parts where feasible;
- distinguish component price from delivery;
- identify counterfeit-prone or specification-uncertain listings;
- prefer realistic available components over the absolute cheapest listing;
- distinguish reusable laboratory tools from purchased project hardware;
- distinguish required, optional, spare and contingency items;
- keep the semester BOM separate from later research hardware;
- provide low, expected and conservative totals;
- retain the ₹3,000 preferred limit and ₹5,000 absolute semester ceiling;
- flag any total that depends on institutional equipment or borrowed parts.

Do not fabricate a market price when a dependable current listing cannot be
found.

For technical specifications, prefer manufacturer data sheets and official
documentation.

## Required hardware detail

For each frozen semester project provide:

- component name;
- required quantity;
- exact role;
- electrical interface;
- supply requirement;
- expected current or power implication;
- why it is selected;
- acceptable substitute;
- procurement risk;
- low, expected and conservative unit price;
- total price;
- reusable, borrowed or purchased classification.

Do not make the future patent-research BOM appear part of the approved semester
budget.

## Team structure

Use placeholders unless the source files establish final names:

- Member 1 — project/technical integration lead.
- Member 2 — firmware, signal processing and data lead.
- Member 3 — hardware, power and physical-rig lead.
- Member 4 — learner/test and inventory assistant.
- Member 5 — documentation, teacher communication and demonstration lead.

For VibeGuard, Member 3 leads the rigid mount, motor rig, power and safety.

For OpenBraille, Member 3 leads CAD, FDM, cams, guides and servo assembly.

Do not assign all important work to one person. Show dependencies, review
responsibilities and backup ownership.

## Semester-roadmap requirements

Prepare a realistic 12-week roadmap for each concept.

Each week must state:

- work;
- owner;
- inputs;
- measurable output;
- exit criterion;
- risks;
- fallback.

For VibeGuard, keep Level 1 semester work separate from future Level 2
attribution research.

For OpenBraille, keep the frozen macro six-dot semester build separate from
future VPD-C7 coupons.

Do not promise patent filing or grant.
