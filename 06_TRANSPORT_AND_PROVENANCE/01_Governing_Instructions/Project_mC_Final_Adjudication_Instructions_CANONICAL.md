# Project mC — Final Cross-Concept Patent-Path Adjudication Instructions

## 1. Role

Act as an independent final review board for Project mC.

Your task is to compare three completed concept-specific patent-evidence records:

- OpenBraille;
- VibeGuard;
- TrueMoist.

Determine:

1. the best three-month semester-build candidate;
2. the strongest technically connected post-MVP patent-development path;
3. the best overall one-project choice under a patent-first but buildability-constrained strategy;
4. the runner-up and fallback;
5. the experiments and kill conditions that must govern the decision.

This is a blind adjudication. Do not use prior-chat context, another adjudication output, excluded concept-swap reports, the supplementary TrueMoist Work Max web audit, unpublished Project mC material not attached, or model agreement as a vote.

Evidence cut-off: 1 August 2026.

This is engineering and patent-strategy decision support, not legal advice, patentability clearance, claim construction, infringement analysis, or FTO clearance. No project or enhancement can be said to “definitely” receive a patent.

## 5. Concept-fidelity tripwires

### OpenBraille

The valid architecture uses six SG90-class servos, PWM actuation, eccentric cams, followers, and vertically translating Braille pins/dots.

The candidate later mechanism associated with the valid synthesis record is the D11-type route involving some combination of a mechanical datum, per-dot calibration, shared isolated current sensing, physical ground truth, bounded retry and fault isolation, and current-aware sequencing.

Do not substitute a moving embossing roller, dimpled roller, encoder wheel, Arduino Mega/RAMPS architecture, continuous paper embosser, or any architecture found only in the failed concept-swap report.

Do not assume the D11 route is already patentable. Audit the conflict between the valid OpenBraille syntheses: one is more conservative and one identifies a bounded Track-B route.

### VibeGuard

The frozen concept is a passive, single-node, local machine-vibration monitor using one rigidly mounted ADXL345-class accelerometer, ESP32-WROOM-32 or ESP32-S3, local time-domain features, triggered FFT, a minimum RMS-plus-persistence classifier, prospective Mahalanobis anomaly scoring, RGB local state, and an isolated-motor Level-1 demonstration.

The strongest later hypothesis concerns single-node target attribution despite neighbour/environmental interference, potentially using independently labelled commissioning states, target and interferer fingerprints, source-ambiguity abstention, contamination-protected baseline updates, remount validity, and speed/load normalization.

Do not substitute a software package-security scanner, secret/configuration scanner, wearable or haptic device, active vibration cancellation, multi-sensor array, audio-only detector, or cloud/RUL platform.

Keep distinct:

- Level 1: isolated motor, normal versus induced abnormal;
- Level 2: target-machine attribution despite independently changing interference.

### TrueMoist

The frozen concept is the validated 72-run local compensation architecture using a genuine analog capacitive soil-moisture probe, nearby DS18B20, controlled salinity-treatment code rather than measured EC, ADS1115 versus calibrated ESP32 ADC1 gate, analog hardening, M0/M1/M2 fixed offline-trained regression, one homogeneous soil, 4 moisture × 3 temperature × 2 salinity × 3 physical replicates, and dry-basis gravimetric truth.

It excludes live EC, cloud, irrigation control, multi-soil MVP, custom multi-frequency sensing, TinyML, and online adaptation.

The strongest later hypothesis concerns physical-reference-bound replacement/remount calibration transfer, contamination-protected validity acceptance, and safe abstention/fallback.

Do not convert TrueMoist into a live-EC or multi-frequency project to improve its ranking.

## 7. Normalize the evidence before ranking

For each concept create two separate fact sheets.

### A. Frozen MVP fact sheet

Report the exact frozen architecture, three-month deliverable, budget, validation plan, primary engineering risk, current patent verdict, strongest prior art, eligibility/FTO concerns, demo value, publication value, and current Track.

### B. Post-MVP invention-development fact sheet

Report the proposed later technical mechanism, relationship to the semester MVP, closest patent and non-patent art, claimed physical technical effect, required experiments, number of units/samples/sites, additional hardware and cost, time beyond the semester, claim usefulness if successful, novelty/obviousness/eligibility/FTO risk, strongest baseline, kill condition, and prospective Track.

Do not compare a frozen MVP from one concept against an aspirational later system from another. Compare like with like.

## 8. Reliability audit of the six selected syntheses

For every synthesis:

- verify concept fidelity;
- identify its current-MVP verdict;
- identify its later-route verdict;
- distinguish verified prior art from inference;
- identify unsupported patent-status or FTO assertions;
- identify internal contradictions;
- explain why disagreements occur;
- assign reliability as high, medium, low, or excluded.

Do not vote or average Tracks.

Where two valid syntheses disagree, adjudicate from claim-focused evidence, project authority, technical specificity, experimental accessibility, and conservative burden of proof.

## 9. Patent comparison principles

Evaluate separately:

1. novelty;
2. inventive step/obviousness;
3. patent eligibility;
4. FTO;
5. enablement/support;
6. useful claim scope.

Do not reward exact part-number differences, low cost by itself, absence of cloud/live EC, adding “AI,” “edge,” “confidence,” or “adaptive” labels, arbitrary thresholds, experimental sample count by itself, model names such as Mahalanobis or ridge, or combining known steps without a demonstrated cooperative effect.

A credible later path requires a specific physical technical problem, a specific mechanism, a measurable technical effect, a nontrivial distinction over the closest art, feasible evidence, useful surviving claim scope, and manageable FTO/design-around risk.

## 10. Semester-build scoring

Score each concept from 0 to 5 on every factor. Provide low/base/high ranges and evidence for each score.

Weights:

- build feasibility within three months: 25%;
- measurement/validation feasibility: 20%;
- budget compliance: 15%;
- live demonstration clarity: 15%;
- schedule and dependency risk: 10%;
- team fit and task divisibility: 10%;
- publication/educational fallback value: 5%.

Calculate a weighted Semester Build Score out of 100.

A numeric score must not replace the narrative. Explain every score difference of at least one point.

## 11. Post-MVP patent-path scoring

Score each concept from 0 to 5 on every factor, again with low/base/high ranges.

Weights:

- distance from closest prior art: 20%;
- specificity of the proposed technical mechanism: 15%;
- inventive-step/obviousness resistance: 15%;
- accessibility of decisive experiments: 15%;
- usefulness and breadth of plausible claim scope: 10%;
- FTO/design-around posture: 10%;
- direct connectedness to the semester MVP: 10%;
- publication value if patentability fails: 5%.

Calculate a weighted Patent-Path Score out of 100.

Penalize paths requiring major redesign, unbudgeted specialist instruments, more units/sites than realistically obtainable, simple baselines likely explaining the benefit, high live-family claim overlap, or a claim center that is generic software/statistics.

## 12. Integrated project selection

The default integrated score is:

- 40% Semester Build Score;
- 60% Patent-Path Score.

Also perform sensitivity analyses at:

- 50% build / 50% patent;
- 30% build / 70% patent.

Do not select a concept solely because it wins one arbitrary weighting.

Report the winner under each weighting, whether the winner is stable, what single uncertain assumption could change the ranking, and whether the correct decision is an immediate selection or a short named feasibility gate before final commitment.

## 14. Evidence and simulation requirements

The requested “simulation” is a structured conditional forecast, not a guarantee.

For every concept provide:

- optimistic case;
- base case;
- pessimistic case;
- broad probability bands as decision-support ranges, not legal probabilities;
- assumptions driving each range;
- one earliest decisive experiment;
- one later decisive experiment;
- estimated patent-path kill probability;
- estimated risk that any surviving claim would be commercially too narrow.

Do not present false precision.
