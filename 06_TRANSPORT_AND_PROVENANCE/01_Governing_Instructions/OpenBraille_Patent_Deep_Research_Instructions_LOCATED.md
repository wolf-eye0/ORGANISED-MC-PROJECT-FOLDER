# OpenBraille Current Patent Verification — Independent Deep Research

## Role

Act as an independent multidisciplinary patent-landscape and embedded-
mechanical engineering research board.

Do not rely on earlier AI patent conclusions as verified truth.

This report will later be compared with three other independently generated
reports. Do not seek consensus and do not assume another model will fill
evidence gaps.

Research current information as of 30 July 2026.

This is technical research, not legal advice.

## Frozen project baseline

OpenBraille’s semester MVP is currently:

- one macro-scale six-dot refreshable Braille cell;
- approximately 300% standard Braille scale;
- ESP32-WROOM-32;
- six SG90-class micro-servos;
- one eccentric cam/follower/pin mechanism per dot;
- direct GPIO PWM;
- FDM-manufactured frame and guides;
- external 5 V supply;
- target budget no more than ₹5,000;
- approximately two-month implementation;
- five-member student team.

The present MVP must not automatically be treated as the patent invention.

The repository currently proposes manufacturing/fabrication and control/
firmware as possible IP directions rather than claiming novelty in the basic
actuator.

## Central research question

Does OpenBraille have a credible, experimentally supportable patent-
development path within its budget and semester constraints?

Determine whether:

1. the frozen SG90/cam/pin architecture already contains any defensible
   technical contribution;
2. a bounded improvement can produce a credible prospective invention;
3. the architecture would require a major redesign;
4. or no credible patent path remains within the project constraints.

Do not declare the concept patentable merely because an exact duplicate is
not found.

## Owner-updated timeline and continuation policy

The current semester implementation window is up to three months, not two
months.

The semester prototype budget remains:

- preferred: ₹3,000 or below;
- absolute ceiling: ₹5,000.

Do not reject OpenBraille solely because the complete patent-bearing
development requires more than three months.

Classify every surviving patent path as:

A. Credible in the current frozen MVP.
B. Credible through a bounded enhancement achievable and testable within
   three months.
C. Semester MVP achievable within three months, but the strongest patent-
   development mechanism requires a later semester or publication-oriented
   research extension.
D. No credible patent-development path established.

For a Track C path, define separately:

- the complete three-month semester deliverable;
- the evidence the semester MVP must generate;
- the later invention mechanism;
- additional experiments, cost, fabrication and duration;
- why the later work remains technically connected to the MVP rather than
  becoming an unrelated project.

Do not treat the three-month deadline as the sole patent-potential filter.
Do not use long-term potential to excuse an unbuildable semester MVP.

## Macro-scale and scale-transfer rule

The frozen semester prototype is approximately 300% standard Braille scale.

Do not assume that success at macro scale proves practical or standard-scale
operation.

For every current or prospective claim element, classify it as:

1. Scale-independent;
2. Scale-sensitive;
3. Standard-scale validation mandatory.

Evaluate explicitly:

- whether macro-scale experiments provide relevant evidence;
- which measured technical effects are likely to transfer;
- which effects depend on dot pitch, cell thickness, actuator dimensions,
  friction, tolerance, force, tactile readability or array density;
- whether the prospective claim would be commercially meaningful if limited
  to macro scale;
- what drawings, tolerance analysis, calculations, simulations or physical
  prototypes would be required to support a practical-scale embodiment;
- whether a standard-scale cell must be built before the patent direction
  can be considered credible.

Do not rate a patent path strong if all meaningful differentiating elements
depend on an unbuilt practical-scale mechanism.
